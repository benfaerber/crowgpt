[ ] The rolling buffer pins the microphone and Record cannot change it

    Capture 107 on 2026-07-30 was recorded on the phone mic with the Rode
    plugged in, the home screen saying so, and the capture claiming
    `inputHonoured = true`. Every part of that is the same bug.

    `WavRecorder.start` (kt:211-217) takes an `input` argument and throws it
    away whenever a listening session is already open — `session.beginRecording()`
    returns true, `beginCapture()` opens a WAV and drains the ring, and nothing
    calls `AudioInputs.choose` or `setPreferredDevice` again. The device was
    pinned once, when `startListening` ran. So:

      - plug the Rode in AFTER the buffer starts and every capture until
        listening restarts goes through the phone mic
      - `onInputsChanged` refreshes the caption immediately, so the screen reads
        "Mic open · VideoMic Me-C" while the open AudioRecord is on BUILTIN_MIC.
        The caption is a hypothesis about the NEXT recording presented as a fact
        about the current one (useRecorder.ts:209-225)
      - `preferredHonoured` still holds the listening session's answer, which
        was `true` — it really did get the built-in mic it asked for at the time.
        Hence honoured=true against the wrong microphone, which is exactly the
        failure the audit predicted and expected to find a different way.

    Capture 106, fourteen hours earlier, is USB_HEADSET with the same 15 s
    pre-roll — the Rode was already in when listening started. That is the whole
    difference between the two.

    THREE FIXES, and they are separable:

      - [x] THE SCREEN NO LONGER LIES. `WavRecorder.activeInput()` publishes what
        the open session is pinned to and what it actually routed to, and
        `domain/micState` is now the single place that turns that into a label
        and a warning. The capture screen and the settings picker both read it
        instead of each deriving their own answer, so the case above shows a
        yellow "Recording on Phone microphone. The list now says VideoMic Me-C —
        a session cannot change microphone once it is open." Nine cases in
        tests/unit/micState.test.ts, including capture 107's exact shape.

        This is the part that mattered: all three derivations agreed the Rode
        was in use, and the one that knew better could not be asked.

      - [x] THE BUFFER NOW ADOPTS A MICROPHONE PLUGGED IN AFTER IT STARTED.
        `onInputsChanged` reopens the listening session on whatever the priority
        list now resolves to — `shouldReopenSession` in domain/micState decides,
        six cases in the test. It costs the ring, which is the honest trade:
        plugging a mic in is an explicit act nearly always followed by wanting
        to record through it, and an empty ring is visible on the button while a
        capture through the wrong mic is visible to nobody. Works in both
        directions, so pulling the Rode out drops back to the phone mic rather
        than leaving a session pinned to hardware that has gone.

        NOT during a capture, deliberately: reopening would end the recording
        and discard what is on disk. So a mic plugged in mid-capture still does
        nothing but warn, and that is the remaining hole.

        SHIPPED BROKEN AND FIXED THE SAME DAY, which is the part worth keeping.
        The first version compared the session's route against the resolved
        preference without noticing the phone's own microphone has two names —
        `type:15` from enumeration, `builtin` from the preference list. So every
        input event read as a device change and reopened the session: four
        restarts in ninety seconds, each discarding the ring, and captures
        reaching back three seconds instead of fifteen. Found in Ben's synced
        log, not by testing. Confirmed working on the Pixel 10 on 2026-07-31.

        The same loop also churned the foreground service, because `setBackground`
        only started it when `isListening()` was already true and an effect
        landing between a stop and a start was told no. That has since been
        fixed and confirmed on a phone (5c51979, 2026-08-01): the wish now lives
        in `BackgroundHold` apart from whether it is granted, and every session
        (re)open reasserts it, so a request refused mid-swap is repaired rather
        than archived. Wanted-but-not-listening deliberately stops nothing —
        mid-swap the service outlives the session on purpose.

      - Record must re-pin, for the capture case only now. A microphone that
        arrives while a capture is running cannot be adopted without ending the
        recording, so the choice is to keep going and say so (what happens
        today) or to split the capture in two. Worth leaving alone until it
        actually bites somebody — the buffer case was the common one.
      - Never report `inputHonoured` without reconciling it against
        `routedDevice`, which WavRecorder already reads at kt:359-366 and never
        compares. A capture whose route is not what was asked for should say so
        even when `setPreferredDevice` returned true — that call registers a
        request, it does not promise a route. `activeInput()` now exposes both
        halves live, so the comparison has somewhere obvious to go.

    Watch for the second, independent path to the same place: `rememberMics`
    pushes BUILTIN before appending newly-seen externals (micPriority.ts:93 vs
    :105) and `resolveMic` short-circuits on it (:162), so a mic seen for the
    first time ranks below the phone and can never be chosen until it is dragged
    up by hand. Symptom that tells them apart: the caption says "Phone
    microphone" rather than the Rode's name, and no warning appears because
    `usingFirstChoice` is true.

[ ] The earbud button's remaining three

    Pulled out on 2026-07-31 of the entry about the button losing to anything
    still playing, so they stop hiding under a checked box — that entry was
    checked because the Spotify case is fixed and confirmed (a blip cannot beat
    an app that is still playing, so we deliberately do not contest mid-
    playback), not because these stopped mattering. Since deleted; the reasoning

    costs.

      - THE PROCESS CAN STILL BE REAPED. There is no `MEDIA_BUTTON` receiver in
        either manifest and `setMediaButtonReceiver` is never called, so once
        the process dies there is no path back — nothing can deliver a press to
        an app that is not running, and every re-claim needs a live process to
        run in. Pocket listening's foreground service is the only thing holding
        it open today. This is the difference between "usually works" and
        "works". Read the FUTURE.txt entry "Waking a killed app with the earbud
        button" before starting: it is researched, and the conclusion was to
        keep the process alive rather than build the receiver, which makes this
        a decision to re-open rather than a gap to fill.

      - THE APP COUNTS ITS OWN CONFIRMATION TONE AS A COMPETITOR. The playback
        callback counts every player and subtracts only our *blips*
        (`blipsInFlight`, MediaButtonPriority.kt:39), so the cue that plays a
        quarter second after every button press reads as "another app started",
        triggers a contest, and then another when it ends. Bounded by the
        cooldown and harmless as far as anyone can tell, but the app fights
        itself twice per press and `othersPlaying` is not telling the truth
        while a cue is sounding. Track the cue in the same counter as the blip.
        The heartbeat makes this continuous rather than twice-per-press.

      - EXPO-AUDIO HOSTS A SECOND MEDIA SESSION IN THIS SAME APK
        (`AudioControlsService`, media3, app manifest line 28). Playing a
        capture back creates a genuinely-playing session in our own uid that
        outranks the trigger's. Reviewing a recording in the field may therefore
        cost the button until the contest re-runs. Not reproduced, but it is the
        one competitor that ships with us.

    And one line of copy: Settings still promises the button unconditionally,
    when the shipped behaviour deliberately yields to an app that is actively
    playing. Say so there.

[ ] Audit the recorder — the core has to be boring and reliable

    Everything else in this app is optional. If a recording fails, the labels,
    the contexts, the sightings and the analysis are all decoration on a thing
    that did not happen — and unlike a lost label, a lost call does not come
    back. The recorder has never had the scrutiny the schema has.

    It has also quietly become the most stateful part of the codebase.
    WavRecorder now juggles `active` / `recording` / `keepListening`, a ring
    buffer behind a lock, a capture thread, and a WAV that may or may not be
    open — and around it sit a foreground service, a microphone priority list
    resolved at the moment of the press, a level-meter subscription that is
    shared between listening and recording, and a listening session that now
    survives leaving the screen. When this entry was written only PreRoll had
    tests.

    SEVEN OF THE NINE FAILURE MODES ARE NOW EXERCISED, and the audit's whole
    premise held: every real bug it found was a *silent* one. Fixed, with tests,
    and the detail is in git rather than here — read the commits touching
    WavRecorder, SessionState and OrphanCaptures. In short: a sticky read error
    spinning a core in a pocket; a full disk handing back a truncated file as
    whole; an abandoned capture thread writing into the next session's WAV; a
    synchronous listInputs freezing the UI at the moment Record was pressed; a
    double tap surfacing as an error over a healthy recording; a killed app
    losing its capture completely; and a revoked microphone saying nothing at
    all. Six JVM test classes and 45 cases now cover the state machine, the
    pre-roll ring, the headset button and the recovery.

    One lesson worth not rediscovering, because the fix for the second-to-last
    of those broke delete within the hour: "no database row points at this
    file" and "a process died writing this file" are different facts, and only
    the recorder knows the second one. It writes a `.writing` marker now.

    WHAT IS ACTUALLY LEFT:

      - PREFERRED MICROPHONE UNPLUGGED MID-RECORDING. Needs the Rode, never
        run, and expected to fail: nothing watches routing changes, so the
        worry that it records `inputHonoured = true` against a different mic is
        live rather than theoretical.

      - STORAGE FULL PARTWAY THROUGH A CAPTURE. Deliberately not run, and not
        to be recorded as passing. The path is fixed and reasoned — a write
        that throws sets `failure`, which stop() reports — but it has never
        executed on hardware. Doing it honestly means filling /data to zero on
        a live handset, which destabilises Android itself. Worth it before a
        long unattended session; not worth a broken phone otherwise.

      - RE-TEST THE KILL on a handset. Recovery is verified by JUnit and was
        confirmed on a phone, but on the build *before* the marker existed, so
        the mechanism that ships is not the one that was seen working.

    The prepareAsync NullPointerException is solved and it was the handles:
    expo-sqlite shares one native connection per path, and garbage collecting
    any JS handle closes it for everyone. One handle now. Not yet confirmed on
    the Pixel 10, which was out of range when the fix landed — if it crashes
    again on swiping back from a capture, this was not the whole story.

[ ] A call can be two things at once — labelling cannot say so

    Capture 104: the third bout is begging and a call together. Today
    `labels.call_type` is one string and the deck replaces rather than adds —
    `decide()` removes any existing label before writing the new one — so
    choosing the second type deletes the first. The recordist has to pick one
    and the other observation is lost.

    FIRST, SEPARATE TWO SITUATIONS, because they want different answers:

      a) TWO BIRDS overlapping. A fledgling begging while an adult calls is two
         vocalisations that happen to share a span, and the honest record is two
         labels — different birds, possibly different ages, certainly different
         sources. The schema already allows this: start_sec/end_sec are free and
         nothing enforces one label per span. What blocks it is only the deck,
         which is built as one card = one decision.

      b) ONE vocalisation that genuinely belongs to two classes. Rarer and more
         interesting, and the case where a single label with two types is right.

    ANSWERED for 104: two birds. The recordist heard a fledgling begging, an
    adult calling, the fledgling continuing, the adult calling again harder, and
    then the begging stopping. So (a) is what to build — a deck that can hold two
    labels on one span — and it needs no schema change at all.

    That also means (b) can wait for a case that actually turns up. Nobody has
    yet produced a single vocalisation belonging to two CORE classes, and
    building for it first would be building for a guess.

    THE PIPELINE IS ALREADY MULTI-LABEL, which is the part worth knowing:

      - corpus.py yields a *set* of CORE classes per segment, not one
      - recover_and_pool.py and build_dataset.py both emit Y as lists
      - export_manifest.py already writes `",".join(sorted(cores))` per file
      - train_american_crow.py computes one-vs-rest AUC per class over the
        multi-label sets, and only narrows to `len(s) == 1` for the shipped
        multiclass head

    So the training side has always expected this and the label capture is the
    narrow part. A head that can say "begging and call" needs the multiclass
    classifier replaced by per-class binary heads, which the AUC numbers in
    train_american_crow.py are already measuring.

    Worth deciding while building: does a two-type label train as two examples
    or one example with two positives? They are different claims, and the second
    is the true one — which is another reason to keep (a) and (b) apart, because
    two birds really is two examples.

[ ] Nothing can record what a call achieved

    Capture 104 is the case, and it is the most interesting recording in the
    corpus for exactly the reason nothing can express: the adult called twice,
    and after the second one the begging STOPPED and did not resume. The outcome
    is the finding. The corpus can say what was said and what was happening
    nearby; it cannot say what happened next.

    Context tags are all simultaneous — dog visible, crows agitated, caller
    stays. Every one answers "what was going on while this call was made". None
    answers "what changed because of it", and that is a different question with a
    different shape: it needs an *after*, and a claim that the two are connected.

    Shapes worth weighing, none obviously right:

      - outcome tags on a label: `other_bird_fell_silent`, `others_left`,
        `bird_approached`. Cheap, composes with what exists, and dishonest in one
        way — a tag on the call asserts the call caused it.
      - a response link between two labels: "this label answers that one".
        Truer to what was heard (adult answering fledgling, twice) and it makes
        the sequence queryable, which single tags never will. More to build.
      - nothing, and keep writing it in notes. Which is what 104 does now, and
        is why the observation is not countable.

    Related and not the same: caller_stays/caller_leaves say what the *caller*
    did, which was the last gap of this kind. This is about what the *other* bird
    did afterwards.

    Worth waiting for a second example before designing. One recording is an
    anecdote, and a schema built from one is usually built wrong.

[ ] A chip for speaking to them — Ben's voice is the experiment, not noise in it

    This is a human–animal communication project and he talks to the crows.
    That is the phenomenon. The vocabulary cannot say it, so every instance is
    currently lost, and the labelling skill was reduced to calling it
    "contamination" in a description — which is both wrong about what it is
    and unreachable by any query.

    Found while labelling on 2026-07-31. "Crows scatter from car" has "hi crow"
    at 46 s, inside a 33-second span labelled `call`, and again at 71 s, inside
    a 3-second span labelled `alarm call` — the alarm is real and his greeting
    is on top of it. Both go into the training manifest today with nothing
    saying so.

    Nearest existing key is `person_shouting` ("Someone shouting or agitated"),
    which is wrong twice: it is not shouting, and it describes a bystander's
    state rather than a known human addressing the birds on purpose.

    Add a key under [people] in config_store.SEED_CONTEXTS with a translation.
    That bumps the config version, and the phone needs a RELAUNCH rather than a
    Sync to see it (refreshConfig runs once per launch, CrowGPTApp.tsx:64).

    What it buys, in the order that matters:

      - The project's own question becomes a query. "Do they answer when I
        speak to them?" is currently unaskable in SQL. With the chip it is a
        join against the next span's call type.
      - Training gets a choice. A span carrying a human voice can be excluded
        from crow-detector training deliberately, or kept on purpose — today
        it is included by accident and nobody knows which spans they are.
      - It separates speaking from standing nearby. `known_passing` already
        says a known human is present; addressing them is a different act, and
        the difference is the whole point.

    Decide before writing it: whether the key means "I spoke" or "any human
    spoke" (the corpus is one recordist today and will not stay that way), and
    whether it carries what was said. Do NOT fold a reply into it — you can see
    a bird react, you cannot see that it answered, which is the same line
    `caller_stays` was named to respect and the same one that keeps detections
    out of `labels`.

[ ] Run the transcriber over the corpus, and see what it says about our own voices

    Ben's, 2026-08-02, and it lands on top of an entry already written: the
    investigation below asks whether anything can FIND human speech in the field
    recordings, and Whisper turns out to be sitting right there having just
    transcribed two field notes correctly. It is a detector we already have,
    already installed, already on the GPU.

    THE OBVIOUS VERSION IS THE WRONG ONE, and it is the same trap the entry
    below names. Whisper is built to return words; handed 116 captures of crows
    it will return words for crows, confidently, because that is the only thing
    it can do. A transcript of a caw is not a null result, it is a false
    positive that reads exactly like a true one. So the measurement has to be
    adversarial from the start:

      - the two known instances first — "hi crow" at 46 s and 71 s in "Crows
        scatter from car". If it misses those, nothing else matters.
      - how often does it emit text over a span already labelled as a crow
        call? That is the number that decides the whole idea. `no_speech_prob`
        per segment is the discriminator worth trusting more than the text.
      - mean_logprob and compression_ratio per segment are already returned by
        transcriber/whisper.py, and hallucinated speech over birdsong should
        look different in both. Test that before believing any transcript.

    WHAT IT WOULD BE WORTH, in the order that matters:

      - Ben's own speech becomes findable in everything already collected. The
        chip entry above only works going forward and only when he remembers he
        spoke; 116 captures have no record at all.
      - The training side gets a choice. A span carrying a human voice can be
        excluded deliberately, or kept on purpose — today it is included by
        accident and nobody knows which spans they are.
      - The privacy answer. This corpus is recorded in public and a detector
        that finds his voice finds a stranger's, which is the prerequisite for
        ever muting one before a corpus is shared.

    DO NOT WRITE THE OUTPUT ANYWHERE A LABELLER READS IT. A transcript of a crow
    call filed as something the recordist said would be the laundering the
    dictation design was built to prevent, arriving through the back door. This
    is a measurement about a recording, like quality.py — not testimony, and not
    an observation.

    Numbered folder in experiments/. A null is a real result here and worth
    writing down: "Whisper cannot tell a caw from a voice" saves the next person
    the afternoon.

[ ] INVESTIGATION: can anything find human speech in this audio automatically?

    The chip above only works going forward, and only when Ben remembers he
    spoke and at what second. Everything already collected — 116 captures —
    has no record at all, and he cannot re-listen to two hours of audio to
    find every "hi crow". A detector would sweep the lot.

    MEASURE FIRST, and the measurement is adversarial rather than
    confirmatory. A caw is loud, broadband and vocal-shaped; a voice-activity
    detector trained on telephony has never been asked to tell one from a
    person, and there is every chance it calls every crow a human. Run it and
    count:

      - does it find the two known instances, "hi crow" at 46 s and 71 s in
        "Crows scatter from car"? Those are the ground truth we have.
      - how often does it fire on a span already labelled as a crow call? That
        is the number that decides whether this is worth building. A detector
        that flags cawing as speech is worse than none, because it would mark
        the corpus's best audio as contaminated.
      - how many kept captures contain speech at all?

    A null here is a real result and worth writing down — "off-the-shelf VAD
    cannot tell a caw from a voice" saves the next person the same afternoon.
    Numbered folder in experiments/, per the usual.

    CANDIDATES, cheapest first. Silero VAD is the one to try first: small,
    CPU, torch — which crowgpt's venv already carries — and built for noisy
    audio rather than clean telephony. WebRTC VAD is cheaper still and almost
    certainly too naive for outdoors. YAMNet has a "Speech" class among its
    521 and would sit closest to how BirdNET already runs. Speaker ID
    (pyannote) is a different and much larger question — knowing *who* spoke,
    not *that* someone did. Do not start there.

    WHERE IT WOULD LIVE if it works: `analyzer/`, alongside quality.py, which
    is the precedent — a per-capture measurement that is deliberately not a
    label, because "a human spoke here" is a property of the recording rather
    than a claim about what a call is. Same rule: pure processing, no server,
    no database, and the worker writes the result.

    THE THIRD REASON TO WANT IT, and it may be the strongest. This corpus is
    recorded in public. A detector that finds Ben's voice finds a passing
    stranger's too, and right now nobody knows which recordings carry a
    conversation that was never meant to be collected. The project already
    censors coordinates and asks about location precision; speech in the audio
    is the same class of question and currently has no answer at all. Knowing
    where it is, is the prerequisite for ever muting it before a corpus is
    shared. That argument stands even if the labelling case does not.

--- 1. The video card's first open — pause once, and say when it cannot open --

Two field reports from that same sweep, fixed in 7bdb684 and not yet re-seen:
the follow rule paused a hand-played picture a beat after every play press
(the audio player reports while paused, and every report re-issued the
pause), and a player that failed to open — the camera can still be releasing
the codec seconds after recording — sat as a silent black box, because the
error branch had no listener and could never render.

  - record a video capture and open it IMMEDIATELY from the list: expect
    either the picture, a "Loading the video…" state, or a said failure with
    a Try again that recovers — never a wordless black box.
  - pause the audio, then play the video with its own controls: it must keep
    playing rather than stopping a beat later. Scrubbing the paused audio
    must not stop it either.

--- 2. The upload loop, once, deliberately ---------------------------------

The fix changed how every upload finishes.

  - record something over ~2 MB and sync it: completes once, does not loop
  - kill the app mid-upload and sync again: resumes rather than restarting

--- 3. The recorder failure modes — what the sweeps have not reached ---------

  - preferred microphone unplugged mid-recording: expect it to say so rather
    than record inputHonoured = true against a different mic. Needs the Rode,
    and expected to fail — nothing watches routing changes today.
  - storage full partway through a long capture: still reasoned, not run, on
    purpose. See above.

--- 4. The video screen's clock -----------------------------------------------

The wait labels and the list placeholder passed the 2026-08-01 sweep; what
remains is the half that matters most and needs a slow fix to see:

  - RECORDEDAT: record a short clip somewhere the GPS fix is slow, and the
    capture's time must be when you pressed Stop, not fifteen seconds later.
    Every capture in the corpus recorded indoors used to be dated by when its
    metadata finished, and nothing on the row ever said so.


[ ] Record the next stranger at a matched distance, and record one of yourself

    Experiment 0016 could not answer whether the crows sounded different at the
    woman on 2026-07-31, for two reasons that are both fixable in the field
    rather than in analysis.

    There is one stranger recording. A fold that holds it out holds out the
    class, so nothing is testable until there are two — and 0006's feasibility
    band wants ten sessions per class before a number means much. Every stranger
    who walks the turf is worth recording, even a boring one.

    And the comparison was 5-20 m against under 5 m, which is what sank it: 9 dB
    of added noise destroyed half the call-rate advantage and most of the
    duration gap. So the stranger recordings worth having are the ones at the
    *same* distance band as an existing recording of yourself. Under 5 m is the
    band with a control already in it (114, "5 crows in shaded flower bed").

    Cheapest version: when you record a stranger, record yourself walking the
    same stretch at the same distance within the hour, the way 113 and 114
    happened to be 32 minutes apart. That pairing is the only reason 0016 could
    run its control at all, and it was luck rather than design.


[ ] The pooled feature cache is stale against the vendored corpus

    Found while confining crow-tools to import_crowtools.py (af69ec0), and it
    predates that change rather than being caused by it.

    `pipelines/ingest/results/pooled_embeddings_crowtools_all-public.npz` holds
    2,494 rows over 1,195 recordings. The corpus resolves 2,339 segments over
    1,098 today. The cache was built when the fork's `.cache/libraries` was
    supplying audio the local download does not have, so it carries ~97
    recordings that are no longer on disk. `experiments/0012/run.py` notices —
    `align()` prints "cached [...] against [...] — rebuild" and refuses the join.

    The gap is almost exactly the 109 library-2 labelled recordings the corpus
    tooling skips without its key. 1,438 of 1,549 labelled recordings are on
    disk; the 2 missing library-1 ones are noise.

    So the order is: have the corpus tooling fetch the labelled audio, then
    `make corpus-build` (half an hour) and `make train-crow`. Worth knowing
    before that last step: the shipped american-crow head was trained off the
    current .npz, so a rebuild moves the model. Compare metrics.json before and
    after rather than overwriting blind — the 0.97 alarm AUC is the number the
    whole corpus argument rests on.

    Until then 0012 cannot be re-run, and `make corpus-build` silently produces a
    smaller cache than the one training last read.


[ ] The analyzer has no loading state — results just appear

    Open a capture, press Analyze, and the screen polls the job every two
    seconds and says "Analyzing… (queued)" with a spinner. Leave the screen
    before it finishes and all of that is gone: `canceled.current` stops the
    poll on unmount (useAnalysis.ts:49-54, :130-148), the job id is a ref that
    dies with the hook, and nothing anywhere is watching it any more. Come back
    later and `loadCalls` fetches whatever is there now, so the detections
    arrive with no history and no warning — hence "it randomly appears".

    THE HOLE UNDER IT is that a running job is unrecoverable state. The phone
    can only poll a job whose id it happens to be holding, so a capture being
    analyzed right now looks exactly like one nobody has ever analyzed:
    "Nothing detected yet." above an enabled Analyze button, which invites a
    second job for audio already in the queue.

    THE SERVER ALREADY KNOWS. `/api/captures/<id>/analyze` sets the capture's
    status to "analyzing" (api/analysis.py:30), and there is a jobs table with
    a capture_id on it. What is missing is any way to ask "what is happening to
    THIS capture" — `/api/jobs/<id>` is by job id only, so the one question the
    screen needs is the one question the API cannot answer. Cheapest fix is to
    return the latest job's state alongside `/api/captures/<id>/calls`, which
    the screen already calls on mount; then re-entering a capture resumes the
    poll instead of starting from nothing.

    WORTH DECIDING WHILE BUILDING: three states, not two. "Never analyzed",
    "queued or running" and "analyzed and found nothing" all render as "Nothing
    detected yet." today, and the third is a real result about the audio while
    the first two are statements about the pipeline. Only the third should ever
    look final.

    AND ONE FOR THE LIST SCREEN, since it is the same fact: a capture that is
    mid-analysis has no marker there at all, so the only way to find out is to
    open it.

[ ] The heartbeat beeps into the buffer while a clip is playing back

    Reported 2026-08-02. Playing a capture back with listening still running
    lets the heartbeat fire into an open microphone, so the tone lands in the
    rolling buffer — and the buffer is what the next capture's 15 s pre-roll
    comes from. A cue that exists to prove the session is alive is writing
    itself into the evidence.

    WORTH SAYING OUT LOUD: THE HEARTBEAT IS THE SMALLER HALF. The playback
    itself goes down the same path. If a clip is played near the phone with a
    session open, the ring now holds a recording of a recording, and nothing
    marks it. The heartbeat is the audible symptom of a wider hole, and a fix
    that mutes only the beep leaves the bigger contamination in place. Decide
    deliberately whether playback should also suspend the ring, or whether the
    pre-roll should be marked as containing playback — do not fix the beep and
    call it done.

    WHERE IT GOES. `Heartbeat.kt` is the pure decision function and already the
    tested seam (`HeartbeatTest.kt`), so add the state there rather than in the
    Handler:

        fun listeningTick(recording: Boolean, listening: Boolean, playingBack: Boolean)
        fun recordingTick(recording: Boolean, playingBack: Boolean)

    Both return null while playingBack, for the same reason a switched-off
    state is a timer that never fires. `RawAudioModule.scheduleBeat` keeps its
    generation idiom untouched; only the `cue()` lambdas learn the new flag.

    The native side does not know playback is happening — `CapturePlayer` and
    `LabelingScreen` drive expo-av from JS. So this needs a small bridge
    (`setPlaybackActive(boolean)` next to `setHeartbeat`), set on play and
    cleared on pause, stop AND unmount. The unmount case is the one that will
    be missed and it fails silently in the worst direction: a flag left true
    mutes the heartbeat for the rest of the session, which is exactly the
    "silence means dead" failure the cue exists to prevent.

    Prior art in the tree: `MediaButtonPriority.blipsInFlight` already counts
    the app's own sounds so they are not mistaken for a competing app. Same
    shape of problem, and worth reading before inventing a second mechanism.

    THE ICON IS NOT DECORATION. The heartbeat's whole contract is that silence
    means something is wrong. Muting it without saying so breaks that contract
    at exactly the moment the user is distracted by playback, so the capture
    screen needs to say "listening, heartbeat muted for playback" — a muted-bell
    state next to the existing mic caption, not a separate warning. `micState`
    is where the screen's single source of truth already lives, so the flag
    belongs there rather than as another derivation.

    Verify on a phone, not in a fixture: play a clip with listening on, then
    record, then check the pre-roll for the tone. A JVM test can assert
    listeningTick returns null and still miss a bridge that never fires.

[x] `_prefix()` returns fewer tokens than `prefix_len` claims, and it has now
    caused the same bug twice

    Done 2026-08-11, the second option: `_prefix` returns a `Prefix` object
    — `written` for the tensor, `stated_len` as the one stripping offset
    (asserted equal to the vocabulary's `prefix_len` at construction), and
    `state()` for callers like complete_fine that write a normally-sampled
    slot themselves without changing the length. All four call sites moved
    off `vocabulary.prefix_len`; tests/test_prefix_seam.py holds the
    invariant across vocabulary shapes. The original entry follows.

    `Sampler._prefix` returns ONE token (the species) while
    `Vocabulary.prefix_len` says two, because the duration bucket is *sampled*
    by the model rather than written. Every caller therefore has to know that
    the first generated token is not a codec id, and slice from `prefix_len`
    rather than from `len(prefix)`.

    Two bugs so far, both silent, both caught only by a round-trip check:

      - `complete_fine` (WP2 arm B) omitted the duration token when building its
        prefix, which shifted every sampled token one stage low. All 40 of them.
        The audio still decoded.
      - 0047's sampling sweep kept the duration token IN its codec stream, which
        shifted the grid the other way and made every arm's churn read 0.99.
        Caught only because the sweep included a baseline arm whose answer was
        already known from 0046.

    The seam is the problem, not the callers. Options, cheapest first:

      - have `_prefix` return the full prefix INCLUDING a duration slot, and let
        the sampler overwrite it when the model picks one. Then
        `len(prefix) == prefix_len` always and the trap closes.
      - or return `(prefix, n_sampled)` so the caller cannot forget.
      - failing either, a docstring on `_prefix` saying it is shorter than
        `prefix_len` and why — it currently explains the opposite risk.

    Worth doing before the next thing that generates tokens: this is the one
    seam in the project that has produced a wrong-but-plausible result twice.

[ ] Look into ISPA — Earth Species' published answer to crowgpt's vocabulary
    question

    Found 2026-08-08 surveying github.com/earthspecies. ISPA (Inter-Species
    Phonetic Alphabet, arXiv 2402.03269, github.com/earthspecies/ispa)
    transcribes animal vocalizations into discrete symbolic sequences — an IPA
    for animals. Two routes: ISP-A works from acoustics directly; ISP-F
    extracts features (MFCC or AVES embeddings), k-means clusters them, and
    maps clusters to phoneme-like symbols, with raw / segmented / phoneme-level
    variants. Pretrained models ship with the repo.

    This is the same problem crowgpt's tokenizer answers with EnCodec codes,
    solved a different way by the group whose whole mission is this project's
    thesis. Worth an afternoon of reading before any more tokenizer work:

      - EnCodec's units were designed for reconstruction of speech and music;
        ISPA's were designed to be short, interpretable and species-agnostic.
        The learnability-vs-ceiling axis that killed 0030, 0035 and 0037 is
        exactly where those designs should differ, and the paper presumably
        reports something about downstream modellability.
      - ISPA symbols are readable. A sitting where Ben looks at two
        transcriptions of the same bout is possible in a way K=8 codec ids
        never were — though ISPA cannot decode back to audio, so it competes
        as an analysis/representation vocabulary, not as crowgpt's generative
        one. Knowing which half it could replace is the point of the read.
      - Their AVES encoder family (BirdAVES: bird-specific, self-supervised,
        MIT, wrapped by github.com/earthspecies/avex alongside BirdNET v2.4
        and Perch v2 behind one API) is the untested third point in the Phase 3
        comparison — general (AST) and species-ID (BirdNET) were measured,
        representation-learning-on-birds never was.

    The outcome is a decision, written down: adopt something, run a comparison
    as a numbered experiment, or decided-not-to with the reason. If it is
    "compare", the cheap first arm is ISPA transcriptions of the bout corpus
    scored with the churn/duty instruments 0053 and 0059 already calibrated.
