[ ] Auto-grading the CrowGPT listening test: what has been measured, and
    the one pilot still worth running (investigated 2026-09-11, Jynx)

    Ben asked whether an AI model could grade the sittings for him. The
    honest state of the instruments, all already in experiments/:

      - BirdNET is circular (0022): it chose the training corpus, so it scores
        crowgpt above real audio. Fair as a ranking of draws (0055), never as
        a verdict.
      - General audio embeddings shrug or invert (0081): AST-FAD put the arm
        Ben ranks worst BELOW the real-vs-real floor; CLAP margins were equal
        to three decimals across arms and across his own defect words.
        Sealed rule: a judge that disagrees with him is a null and never
        pre-screens a pool.
      - A judge distilled from his own 304 grades (0082) reaches pool-held-out
        Spearman 0.48 (0.33-0.57 across five pools), catch-direction accuracy
        0.50-0.86. Positive, modest, carried by the +/-2 trials. Its sealed
        voice-0078 call (+0.087 toward calltype, 55%) is still unscored
        because that sitting sits at 16 of 48.

    Why none of these replaces him yet: the distinction he grades ("hoarse",
    "blowing dust", "robotic", "crow shape but not real") is textural and
    lives below what corpus-trained embeddings encode, and the labelled set
    is 322 answers, 15 of which carry a defect tag. The catch trials are the
    only ground truth with an external answer; everything else is his ear.

    THE UNTRIED THING is an audio-language model as judge: Qwen2-Audio-7B (or
    Qwen2.5-Omni) runs on the 16 GB card in bf16/4-bit; a hosted model with
    audio input is the other route. Claude does not take audio, but a vision
    model reading mel spectrograms is a legitimate variant: spectrograms are
    how bioacousticians judge, and dust (0069: low periodicity under a normal
    envelope) is visible on one. The pilot costs nothing new: score every
    trial in the five completed pools with the same prompt Ben answers
    (reference real + two clips, -2..+2 toward the first, name defects from
    the fixed vocabulary), then run 0082's harness on the outputs: per-pool
    Spearman against his margins, catch-direction accuracy, and the sealed
    voice-0078 / capacity-0086 prediction BEFORE he finishes those. Promote
    only on the 0081 rule. A judge that clears ~0.7 Spearman and 90% on
    catches earns the pre-screen role: ranking checkpoints and killing the
    arm nobody would ship, so his 48 trials go to the close pair that needs
    them. Nothing at any score earns the verdict role; 0038's 8.3-point
    overnight drift on identical weights says even his own ear is not a
    stable yardstick, and the pool system's blinding, dosing and reveal_at
    exist because of that, not around it.

    Expected shape of the null: the model narrates plausible crow-talk with
    grades uncorrelated to his. CLAP's flat shrug is the prior. Record it in
    experiments/ either way.

    THE HUMAN-CROWD VARIANT (Ben, 2026-09-12): a data-labelling firm through
    its API. Same validation move, different judge. What the crowd can do is
    the coarse question, "which is less robotic", at many raters per trial,
    which averages out the drift and side bias a single ear carries (0038,
    0053). What it cannot do is name a defect or hear a close pair: the
    project's best findings came from Ben's descriptions, and 0067 shows even
    his ear returning 0.44 then 0.57 on the same close comparison. Qualify
    raters on the catch trials the pools already contain (real vs generated
    is the one thing any ear should manage; Ben is 39/46) and drop anyone
    below ~80%. Calibration first: send churn-spread-0053, already graded,
    and see whether the pooled crowd reproduces its 0.92 / 0.82 / 0.55
    dose-response. Reproduce it and the crowd earns the pre-screen role;
    miss it and the crowd is a null like FAD and CLAP. Two practical gates:
    the listen page cannot be exposed (no TLS, one shared secret, home
    network), so trials go out and answers come back through the firm's API;
    and listening_answers has one answer per trial (UNIQUE trial_id), so
    multi-rater needs a rater column or one pool per rater. Rough cost is
    tens of dollars per 48-trial pool at typical per-judgment rates; confirm
    against the firm's price sheet before counting on it.

[ ] The raw-audio audit's lesser findings — fixed the top three, parked the rest

    A full read of the Kotlin audio module (2026-07-30) found ten things. The
    three that touched field recording shipped: stale failure crossing
    sessions, a dead capture thread leaving the session claiming to be alive,
    and OnDestroy leaking the recorder. The rest, in rough order of worth:

      - Cross-thread races: ListeningService.onTaskRemoved calls
        stopListening() on the main thread while everything else runs on the
        module queue, and WavRecorder's plain fields have no protection;
        MediaButtonPriority mixes module-thread claim/release with main-thread
        heartbeat state the same way. SessionState is synchronized now; the
        rest is not. Narrow windows, real races.
        The microphone swap (2026-07-31, MicrophoneSwap.kt) adds one more
        window of the same class: a swipe-away mid-swap runs stopListening()
        on the main thread while the module queue is between close and reopen.
        Noted, not fixed here — whatever fixes the race above covers it.
      - awaitThread() nulls the thread reference even when join(3s) times out,
        which quietly bypasses awaitPreviousThread's refuse-a-second-microphone
        guard for a read stuck longer than that.
      - probeAudioSources has no guard against a live session — diagnostics
        mid-capture is the two-AudioRecords coin toss on purpose.
      - recoverOrphans hardcodes 48000/frameBytes=2; the recovered file's own
        header at offset 24 knows the real rate.
      - start() while listening silently ignores its sampleRate/input args.
      - WavFile.close() casts dataBytes to Int — a clean close corrupts the
        header past 2 GB, ~6.2 hours at 48 kHz mono. OrphanCaptures.repair
        has the same casts.
      - minSdkVersion says 24 but ListeningService uses API-26 calls
        unguarded. Moot on this fleet; the floor is a lie.
      - Dead code: ListeningService.running is written and never read.
        OrphanCaptures.scan deletes the marker even for a header-only file it
        declines to recover, stranding a 44-byte WAV in cacheDir forever.

    None of these blocks a field session. Revisit the concurrency one if the
    recorder grows another caller thread, and the probe guard before handing
    the diagnostics screen to anyone else.

[ ] Tier audio off the VPS to a NAS once the corpus is big enough to care

    Measured basis: a real field recording averages 34 s / 3.28 MB, so two a
    day per contributor is 2.4 GB per person per year, permanently. Storage is
    the only cost that grows — CPU is 10 seconds a day per user and bandwidth
    is noise.

    Worth doing past roughly 1 TB, and not before. A 4-bay NAS with disks is
    ~$800 up front plus a couple of dollars of power a month; object storage
    is ~$0.006/GB/month. Below a terabyte the NAS never pays back — 240 GB
    (100 contributors, year one) is $3.60/month in the cloud, which is less
    than the electricity.

    The schema is already shaped for this and it was not planned: `media` has
    `storage`, `storage_ref` and `sha256`, every one of the 86 rows is hashed,
    and `storage` is 'local' everywhere. Tiering is a new storage value and a
    mover, not a migration.

    Design constraints, in the order they matter:

      - The NAS *pulls*. Nothing at home listens on a public port; the VPS
        never needs to know the house exists. Tailscale or a WireGuard tunnel,
        outbound only.
      - Delete from the VPS only after re-hashing the copy at the far end and
        matching `sha256`. A mover that trusts its own success is how a corpus
        loses a file quietly.
      - RAID is not a backup. A corpus that exists only in one house is one
        flood, theft or fire from gone, and the field recordings are the part
        that cannot be re-collected. Keep an offsite copy of the kept captures
        at minimum — that is a few gigabytes and costs pennies.
      - The database, the envelopes, the detections and the labels stay on the
        VPS whatever happens. They are small (20 KB a capture) and they are
        what makes the dashboard work while the house is offline.
      - Nothing upstream may depend on the archive being reachable. The same
        rule as push-only sync, one level up: the phone does not depend on the
        server, and the server must not depend on the NAS.

    FLAC is measured and shipped — experiments/0007 — and it moves this a long
    way out. Bit-exact on all 77 files; real field recordings come out at 22.4%
    of WAV rather than the ~50% the brochures promise, because quiet audio
    carries less entropy. 2.39 GB per contributor-year becomes 0.54, so the 1 TB
    threshold above is about 1,850 contributor-years away: a thousand people
    recording for eighteen months.

    `media.pcm_sha256` exists for this: a hash of the samples rather than of the
    file, so a mover can verify a copy at the far end before deleting an
    original even though the container changed.

Call-type confidence is not confidence — calibrate it or stop showing it

    Measured in experiments/0008: across all 187 detections on the server, the
    call-type probability correlates with species confidence at r = +0.03, and
    32% of them sit at exactly 1.000. The saturated third skews to *lower*
    species confidence (0.654 against 0.779) — the opposite of what a
    calibrated head does.

    The mechanism is ordinary: a logistic regression on standardised BirdNET
    embeddings will return 1.000 for input that falls outside anything it was
    trained on, and it was trained on archive recordings, not a phone in a
    street next to a lawn mower. So p is closer to a distance-from-training-set
    measure than a confidence.

    It matters because the number is shown to the recordist and reads as
    certainty. Capture 97 at 15-18s: species confidence 0.171, call type
    "alarm call", p = 1.000.

    Options, roughly in order of effort: Platt scaling or isotonic regression
    on held-out field audio; abstaining below a species-confidence floor rather
    than labelling anyway; or simply not displaying p until one of those
    exists. The last is free and honest.

    Revisit when there are enough human labels to calibrate against — today
    there are 14, which is not enough to fit anything.

    Since 2026-07-30 the stored SNR to calibrate against exists: every capture
    carries a `capture_quality` row, written at analysis and backfilled over
    the corpus.

A merged capture has no honest band SNR — filters have to remember

    Found by the quality backfill on its first pass: a merge holds digital
    zero in the gaps between its parts, so the quiet percentile the SNR ratio
    divides by is exactly 0 and the number is infinity. `band_snr_db` is NULL
    for those rows on purpose — floors and levels are stored, the ratio is
    not, and NULL means "no quiet signal to measure against" rather than
    "unmeasured" (captures 89, 99 and 100 today, and 100 is kept).

    The consequence is for any future filter of the "kept captures above
    15 dB" kind: a plain `WHERE band_snr_db >= 15` silently drops merged
    captures, which are disproportionately the interesting ones — merging is
    something a recordist only bothers doing to a good morning. Decide per
    query whether NULL means excluded or needs its parts measured separately.

Neural denoising of field captures — decided not to

    experiments/0031 measured biodenoising (Earth Species Project, ICASSP 2025)
    on the four mower captures and it erased the crow: BirdNET went from a
    confident American Crow detection to nothing at all on 3 of 4, and the one
    survivor was the capture with the *highest* band SNR to begin with. A
    denoiser whose failures concentrate on the noisy recordings is no use, since
    the noisy ones are the whole reason to want one.

    Two things worth keeping from it. The 8 kHz ceiling was not the cause —
    0003 rejected the crow-tools denoiser on bandwidth, and that objection was
    true but not operative; a resample-only control kept 4/4 detections, so the
    damage is what the model removes inside the band it keeps. And band SNR
    would have said the opposite: it more than doubled on every capture, 19 to
    44 dB on 94, while the detections vanished. Anything that gates the quiet
    passages inflates a 99th-over-10th-percentile ratio by construction.

    Revisit if a >16 kHz checkpoint appears, or for the generative route rather
    than field detection — the BirdDiff note further down is about learning from
    the archive corpus, which 0031 does not test and does not close.

A way for the server to fill in blanks on the phone, without breaking push-only

    The asymmetry, met repeatedly on 2026-07-28/29: Claude annotates captures
    server-side — scene notes, counts, distance, observer role, family, the ★
    Keep flag — and none of it can ever reach the handset. Sync is push-only and
    Restore is additive-only, so there is no path at all. Captures 94-97, 100,
    102 and 103 are all rich on the server and blank on the Pixel 10.

    Worse than invisible, it is fragile: edit one of those captures on the phone
    and push, and the blank fields overwrite the annotated ones. The metadata is
    not merely unshown, it is one tap from being erased by a client that never
    knew it existed.

    Decided NOT to build a general pull. "Which side wins" needs a per-field
    answer, and push-only exists precisely so nobody has to give one. A sync
    that can overwrite in both directions can overwrite the wrong way round, and
    the thing it would overwrite is a field recording.

    The shape that looks safe is Restore's rule applied one level down. Restore
    never touches a capture the phone already has, because a phone that lost one
    has no truth to contradict. The same logic works per *field*: the server may
    fill a field the phone has left null, and may never change one it has set.
    Nothing is overwritten, so there is no conflict to resolve and no rule to
    get backwards.

    That gives the useful case for free — a capture the recordist never
    described picks up the description — and refuses the dangerous one by
    construction. It also stays honest about who said what, which matters here:
    a count Claude inferred from a chat message is not the same evidence as one
    the recordist tapped in at the time, and the corpus should not forget which
    it has.

    So it wants, roughly:
      - GET /api/captures/since/<revision> or similar, returning field values
      - the phone applying only where its own value is null and the capture has
        no unsynced edits
      - a provenance marker per field, or at least per capture, saying the value
        came from the server rather than the field
      - WIRE_VERSION bump, and REQUIRED only once the phone actually sends
        something new

    Revisit when annotating server-side stops being occasional. Today it is
    cheap to accept the asymmetry, because the server is where analysis,
    training and export_manifest read — the metadata is already where it does
    its work, and the phone showing less than the server knows costs nothing but
    surprise.

Starting a recording without taking the phone out

    Wanted: a trigger reachable from a pocket, so a bird that calls while the
    phone is away is not lost to fifteen seconds of fumbling. Two shapes were
    proposed — a physical button, or a spoken "record" with a spoken
    confirmation in headphones. Neither is being built yet; this is the
    weighing.

    THE WORKFLOW, which decides most of this. A crossbody bag holds the
    sunglasses, the peanuts, the mic and the wind guard, and during a recording
    the phone rests in the bag with the VideoMic Me-C exposed and aimed outward
    from the chest. It works well. The mic is therefore *mounted*, not merely
    carried — and that is the thing a remote protects.

    Which means the cost of the on-screen button is not the seconds. It is that
    reaching in, lifting the phone, unlocking it and pressing takes the mic off
    aim, moves it while it is live, and puts it back somewhere slightly
    different. Experiment 0010 measured one to two handling transients per
    capture at full scale; a phone being lifted out of a bag and replaced is the
    obvious candidate. So the trigger is not a convenience here, it is what lets
    a deliberately aimed microphone stay aimed.

    That also raises the cost of a spoken trigger again: a chest-mounted mic
    pointing outward has the recordist's mouth close and near-on-axis, which is
    the worst possible geometry for keeping a voice out of the audio.

    NEXT, THE REFRAME. Pre-roll already solves part of the problem and it is
    easy to forget. With listening on, the app holds a rolling buffer — up to
    15 s today — so a capture started *after* a call still contains it. The
    requirement is therefore not "trigger before the bird" but "trigger within
    the buffer", which is a far weaker thing to build for. Before either option
    below, ask whether a longer buffer plus the existing on-screen button is
    enough. 30-45 s of ring costs memory and nothing else, and it needs no new
    hardware, no new permission and no new failure mode.

    That also changes how to judge the options: latency and reliability of the
    trigger matter much less than whether it fires at all.

    OPTION A — a physical button

      Cheapest real version is a Bluetooth camera shutter remote, about £10,
      which presents as an HID device sending a media or volume key. Android
      delivers those to a MediaSession even with the screen off and the app
      backgrounded, which is the same mechanism headset buttons use — so this
      is a supported path rather than a hack.

      For:
        - deterministic. It fires when pressed and never otherwise, which
          matters more than it sounds: a spurious capture is not free, it is a
          row in a corpus whose whole discipline is knowing what is real.
        - silent. No talking to a phone in public, and nothing added to the
          audio.
        - works with cold hands, gloves, through fabric.
        - negligible battery, no continuous computation.
        - already-solved failure modes: the foreground service and mic
          priority work is done.

      Against:
        - another object to carry, charge and lose.
        - pairing drops. A remote that silently disconnected is a trigger that
          looks fine and does nothing — needs a connection indicator, or it
          becomes the third silent failure in this app's history.
        - intercepting *volume* keys specifically is fragile across Android
          versions; media keys via MediaSession are the stable route.

    OPTION B — a spoken trigger

      Against, and one of these is close to disqualifying:

        - THE VOICE LANDS IN THE CAPTURE. The mic is the field recorder. Saying
          "record" puts the recordist's voice into the pre-roll buffer and
          therefore into the audio, a few seconds before the call of interest.
          Capture 102's notes already have to warn that "hi crows" is not a
          crow call. A trigger that reliably contaminates every capture it
          starts is working against the recording it makes.
        - speaking also changes the birds. These are habituated crows a few
          metres away that respond to the recordist; a spoken command is an
          event in the scene, not a neutral control.
        - false triggers. Keyword spotting on a street with machinery and crows
          will fire on something eventually, and each one is a junk capture to
          find and delete.
        - no Android API for custom hotwords. Means Porcupine (commercial
          licence), a TFLite keyword model to train and tune, or Vosk (heavy).
          All are real work, and all run continuously.
        - battery and thermal, for an hour, in a pocket. The recorder audit
          lists exactly that as never having been tested even *without*
          continuous inference.

      For:
        - nothing to carry.
        - hands stay free, which matters when the other hand holds binoculars.

    ON THE SPOKEN CONFIRMATION — prefer haptics

      "Recording starting now" in the headphones has two problems. The port is
      taken: the VideoMic Me-C occupies the phone's only USB-C socket, so app
      audio must go over Bluetooth or through the mic's own monitor jack, and
      the monitor jack carries the input rather than the phone's output. And
      speech at the moment recording starts is speech in the first seconds of
      the capture, whichever route it takes.

      A vibration pattern does the same job with none of that: distinct pulses
      for started and stopped, silent, no headphones needed, nothing added to
      the audio, and it works in a pocket by design. If audio confirmation is
      still wanted later, a short non-speech tone over Bluetooth is the version
      that does not put words in the recording.

    RECOMMENDED ORDER, when this comes up again

      1. Media-key trigger via MediaSession, tested with a cheap BT remote
         clipped to the bag strap, with a visible connection state so a dead
         remote cannot fail silently. Promoted to first because of the bag: the
         remote is what keeps an aimed microphone aimed, which no amount of
         buffer does. The "another thing to carry" objection also mostly
         evaporates — the bag is already being carried, and a shutter remote on
         the strap is lighter than the peanuts.
      2. Haptic confirmation on start and stop. Needed *more* with a remote than
         without: a button with no feedback and the phone out of sight is a
         trigger you cannot tell has worked.
      3. Lengthen the pre-roll ring anyway. Still free, still reversible, and it
         covers the gap between hearing a call and finding the button.
      4. Spoken trigger only if 1-3 prove insufficient, and then knowing that a
         chest-mounted mic puts the recordist's mouth close and on-axis.

    Decided not to: voice as the *first* thing to build. It is the most
    appealing and the most expensive, and it fights the recording.

    THE CHOSEN KEYWORD, if it is built: "Ok Garmin, Video speichern", with a
    spoken "Recording now" back. Ben's reason is that it is funny — it is the
    German dashcam command, and shouting it is a meme. Fine as a reason; a
    feature nobody enjoys using does not get used. Three notes on it:

      - A GERMAN PHRASE IS HARDER, NOT EASIER. Off-the-shelf on-device keyword
        spotting is trained on English. Porcupine can generate a custom wake
        word including German, but that is the commercial tier; a TFLite model
        needs recordings of the phrase to train on, and "speichern" has a final
        consonant cluster that a street with a leaf blower will eat. Expect to
        tune a threshold against real field audio rather than a quiet room.
      - "OK GARMIN" IS SOMEBODY'S TRADEMARK. Irrelevant while this is one
        person's app on two handsets — and the "goes viral" plan is the one
        scenario where it stops being irrelevant, because it only goes viral if
        it is published. Worth remembering at that point, not before.
      - THE JOKE AND THE PROBLEM POINT THE SAME WAY. A long, loud, distinctive
        phrase is exactly what keyword spotting wants: four syllables of
        structure beats "record", which a crow's rattle can plausibly trip. So
        the funny option is also the technically better one.

    What it still costs, unchanged by the keyword: the phrase lands in the
    pre-roll and therefore in the capture, seconds before the call of interest,
    spoken into a chest-mounted mic at close range and near-on-axis. And
    "Recording now" spoken back adds a second voice event at the moment
    recording starts. If this is built, the confirmation should be haptic even
    if the trigger is spoken — the trigger has a reason to be audible, the
    acknowledgement does not.

    Not started because it needs a decision that is not mine: Porcupine's
    licence, or the work of training and tuning a custom model. Either is a real
    commitment rather than an afternoon, and the pre-roll and remote options
    above deliver most of the benefit for a fraction of it.

    POSTSCRIPT 2026-08-15: the option this entry never listed — an acoustic
    level trigger — got built, as idle mode, for a case this entry never
    imagined: the phone indoors, the mic on the fire escape, nobody's hands
    anywhere. It reuses this entry's central reframe (trigger within the
    buffer, not before the bird) and answers the spurious-capture objection
    with a synced `trigger = "auto"` column, blank role/distance, and BirdNET
    validation after upload. The band gate is 0013's: 800–4000 Hz, 12 dB over
    a measured floor. None of that changes this entry's question — a *walk*
    still wants a deliberate trigger, because an armed threshold on a moving
    street would fire all day, and the media-key remote remains the right
    first build for the bag. Same wish, two different instruments.


[ ] Split alarm into mobbing vs alerting — NOT waiting on labels any more.
    Experiment 0036 (2026-08-02) re-answered the blocking question and the
    answer inverts this entry's plan.

    mob's average precision does not move with labels. Across a tenfold range
    it sits flat — 0.769 at 225 segments, 0.706 at 2,338 — with prevalence
    steady between 0.29 and 0.34, so this is not the prevalence artifact AP
    curves are prone to. The fitted ceiling is 0.719 and AP 0.80 comes back
    unreachable by adding labels of this kind at all.

    So "label mobbing in the field first, then split" was right about the order
    and wrong about the reason. Field mobbing labels are still worth collecting,
    but as a TEST set — something that can falsify the 0.71 — not as training
    volume, which is the thing 0036 shows is not missing.

    The lever is the backbone. 0025 already measured it: Perch lifts mob-vs-alert
    from 0.637 to 0.760 on the same corpus while birdnet-centred drops it to
    0.540. Revisit this split after a backbone decision, not after a labelling
    push.

    Everything below still holds — the end state is still a replacement, and the
    three provenance changes still went in ahead of it.

    WHAT 0012 MEASURED, and why the split is worth making at all. It asked
    whether the head should predict all seven crow-tools call-type flags instead
    of the four CORE classes they collapse into. Mostly no, and the "mostly" is
    one pair:

      - scold: 0.581 grouped AUC against a 0.947 control on the same features,
        76% of it also flagged mob, and only *asked* on 1,089 of 2,494 segments.
        Decided not to — three reasons and no counterweight.
      - grief: 3 segments in one recording. Decided not to.
      - song and call: nothing to expand. softSong is the only flag mapping to
        song and rattle the only one mapping to call, so those CORE classes are
        renames, not collapses.
      - mob vs alert: real. 0.813 grouped, and 0.726 inside the 41 recordings
        that carry both — where the recording is held constant and cannot be the
        answer — against 0.848 for a known-acoustic control pair on the same
        test. A genuine acoustic difference, thin at 138 segments.

    WHY IT IS CHEAP. Trained on the six flags and mapped back down to CORE, one
    multiclass head scores 0.843 macro-F1 against 0.842 for the same head trained
    on CORE directly, same rows and folds. The finer split costs nothing at the
    level the app displays.

    THE END STATE IS A REPLACEMENT, NOT AN ADDITION. Decided: when this happens,
    `alarm call` stops being a CORE class and mob and alert take its place. No
    second-stage head sitting over a coarse one, no phone showing the old word
    while a finer answer waits in a side channel, no flag choosing between them.
    Two vocabularies for the same distinction is the thing that rots — every
    count has to say which one it used, and eventually one of them is wrong.

    So the order is: fix the provenance, relabel, then replace the class. The
    first is done (see below). The second is the actual work and it is manual —
    16 existing `alarm call` labels get re-heard and re-answered as mob or alert.
    The third is then a CORE_CLASSES change with a WIRE_VERSION bump, and the
    seeded field labels `mobbing`/`scold` collapse into it rather than mapping
    down to something that no longer exists.

    WHAT WENT IN AHEAD OF IT, so the relabelling does not destroy what it
    replaces:

      - a re-answered label keeps its old answer in `label_revisions` instead of
        overwriting it, and its config_version moves with the new answer. The id
        does not move, so a label's witnessed context stays attached. This is what
        makes "did the finer list change anybody's mind" answerable afterwards —
        the comparison needs both answers and both vocabularies.
      - the export carries the word the human chose (`labels`) next to the CORE
        class it folds into (`type`). A finer head selects the rows that said the
        finer word; the coarse rows are absent from it rather than sitting in it
        as negatives. That last part is the whole lesson of 0012 — crow-tools
        scored `scold` at 0.581 partly by reading 1,405 unanswered segments as no.
      - `label_defs.version_introduced` is now recorded when a label is added to a
        running fleet rather than defaulting to 1, which is what says whether a
        word was on the menu when somebody chose a different one.

    NOT DONE, deliberately: backfilling the 15 labels whose config_version is
    NULL. Migration 0010 already decided against it and was right — the phone
    labels offline against a cached vocabulary, so `created_at` arithmetic gives
    the wrong answer and would write a guess in as a fact. It is also unnecessary:
    created_at bounds the version from *above*, and any future split is introduced
    at a higher version, so a 2026-07-26 label provably predates it either way.
    NULL keeps meaning "unknown", and unknown is never read as a negative.

    THE LABELS ARE A TEST SET, NOT THE BLOCKER. This paragraph used to say the
    split was waiting on them; 0036 inverted that, and the preamble above is the
    corrected version. Still worth collecting, and still absent: 0 of 67 human
    labels use `mobbing` or `scold`, both on offer since version 1, while 34 of
    those 67 are `alarm call` and would each want re-hearing. One in four alarm
    calls lands on the wrong side of the line (mob called alert 124/595, alert
    called mob 126/475), which is fine as a hint and not fine as a claim. What
    field labels CAN do is falsify the 0.71 ceiling; what they cannot do is raise
    it, because AP is flat across a tenfold range of training labels.

    And the thing that turned up alongside it, which matters more: the corpus's
    `call` class is a rattle detector. Contact calls are the most common crow
    vocalisation and the corpus contains none, so the class the field will
    exercise hardest is the one trained on something else. A collection problem,
    not a taxonomy one.

[~] CrowGPT — synthesising a caw, which the corpus can already almost pay for

    PARTLY BUILT, 2026-07-30. The second REVISIT trigger below fired — the
    unconditioned "does it sound like a crow at all" demo — and it is now
    crowgpt/, with experiment 0014 as the codec gate in front of it. Everything
    about call-type conditioning in this entry still stands untouched and is
    still the reason this is not finished.

    What the build changed about the numbers here: the ~65,000 units became
    25,227 three-second BOUTS totalling 12.75 hours, because training on
    isolated 0.4 s calls would mean inventing the gaps between them at
    evaluation time, which is exactly where realism is being measured. A quarter
    of the raw bouts were dropped for not landing in a corvid window.

    0013 measured the thing this needs and the answer was better than expected:
    61.6% of the 40.8-hour archive corpus is a corvid at the pipeline's own
    threshold, which is 24.5 hours, and it cuts into roughly 65,000 discrete
    call units at a median 0.376 s with no human labelling involved at all. That
    is enough to fine-tune a pretrained audio generative model, and probably
    enough to train a small unconditional one on 0.4 s units from scratch.
    Encoding is a non-issue: 96.8% of units roll off under 8 kHz, so 16 kHz
    loses nothing and the 26.5 lossy hours are as good as the 14.3 lossless.

    So the audio is not the blocker, which is the opposite of how this looked.
    Unconditional and species-conditioned synthesis are buildable now, and
    species conditioning is free because BirdNET's species ID is what selected
    the windows in the first place.

    What is missing is the ability to *ask for a call type*, and that has a
    structural shape rather than a size:

      - Human ground truth totals 67.7 seconds of call audio across 35 labels,
        of which exactly 5 isolate a single call. Every label is a 3-second
        BirdNET window, median 3 call units wide, one of them 41. Fine for a
        classifier, nearly useless for a synthesiser, which needs to point at
        one call.
      - The audio and the ability to label it are anti-correlated by species.
        The only head good enough to pseudo-label with is American Crow (AUC
        0.904-0.975) and it sits on 4,324 units, 6% of the pool. The three
        deepest pools are Rook (19,493 units, AUC 0.478-0.757), Jackdaw (17,461
        units, NO head at all) and Raven (15,271, AUC 0.579-0.809).

    So the work is not collection. It is a call-type head for Jackdaw, and
    better ones for Rook and Raven — at which point ~50,000 units become
    conditionable and this stops being a question.

    REVISIT WHEN there is a call-type head for the deep-pool species. The other
    trigger — an unconditioned demo for its own sake — has been taken, and it
    did need nothing that was not already on disk.

    Note the trap if anyone reuses 0013's segmentation: the burst detector
    passes its control at 12 dB (density and duty cycle both rise monotonically
    with BirdNET confidence) and fails it at 6 dB, where density goes flat and
    the units are mostly background. Start at 12. crowgpt/segment.py already
    does, and adds the cap the original lacks — 3% of bouts are a single
    unbroken burst running past 3 s, one of them 23 s, and left whole they fall
    outside any fixed context window and vanish without a word.

    A CEILING WORTH KNOWING BEFORE ANYONE JUDGES THE OUTPUT. 0014 measured what
    the tokenizer costs: at 6 kbps, EnCodec round-tripping real audio keeps 81%
    of BirdNET's corvid detections against a 98.7% ceiling for resampling alone.
    So generated audio cannot score above ~0.81 retention no matter how good the
    model gets, and anything comparing it against untouched recordings is
    blaming the model for the codec. 12 kbps buys 0.877 for double the sequence
    length, which is the upgrade to make first if the model ever saturates.


[x] Waking a killed app with the earbud button — researched, and decided not to

    Asked for directly on 2026-07-30, researched against AOSP rather than
    recollection, and the answer is "possible, on an undocumented mechanism,
    behind a refactor we should not do casually". Writing it down so nobody
    re-derives it.

    IT DOES WORK, AND NOT FOR THE REASON YOU WOULD EXPECT. The documented
    foreground-service exemption list does not mention media buttons at all, and
    the `microphone` service type is explicitly documented as impossible to
    start from the background. Both are true. It works anyway because
    `MediaButtonReceiverHolder.send()` in system_server attaches a
    `setTemporaryAppAllowlist(..., REASON_MEDIA_BUTTON)` for ten seconds AND
    `setBackgroundActivityStartsAllowed(true)` to the broadcast; the second of
    those is what `ActiveServices.shouldAllowFgsWhileInUsePermissionLocked`
    falls through to when the documented reasons all deny. So the mic service
    starts because of a background-activity grant, which is nowhere near where
    anyone would look.

    The privilege is dropped the moment `onReceive` returns, so
    `startForegroundService` must be called synchronously inside it — no
    coroutine, no handler post.

    USE `setMediaButtonBroadcastReceiver(ComponentName)`, NOT the PendingIntent
    overload, which is deprecated and worse than deprecated here: API 31+ forces
    FLAG_IMMUTABLE or FLAG_MUTABLE, and immutable means the system's
    `EXTRA_KEY_EVENT` is discarded when it fills in the intent — the press
    arrives with no way to tell which key it was.

    TWO PRECONDITIONS THAT DECIDE WHETHER IT IS WORTH IT:

      - You only become the remembered media button receiver if you have
        actually PLAYED audio while the session was active. A recording app that
        never makes a sound is never eligible. We happen to satisfy this already
        — MediaButtonPriority's silent blip is doing double duty and nobody
        designed it to.
      - The remembered receiver is consulted ONLY when no live session exists
        anywhere on the phone. Any other app holding an active session — Spotify
        paused mid-album counts — takes the press instead. On a daily driver
        that is the common case, not the edge case.

    THE REAL BLOCKER IS OURS, NOT ANDROID'S. A cold-started service would have
    to record, and `WavRecorder` is instantiated per module instance rather than
    per process. A service that made its own would be a second recorder against
    one microphone, and reconciling that means making the recorder a
    process-wide singleton — the most safety-critical class in the app, the one
    every entry in TODO's audit says to leave alone unless there is a reason.
    Doing that to cover a case pocket listening already covers is the wrong
    trade.

    DECIDED NOT TO, for now. The robust answer is the opposite one: keep the
    process alive so the press lands on the live-session path, which is
    well-trodden and is what actually works today. Pocket listening already does
    this whenever it is on.

    REVISIT WHEN there is a reason to want the trigger armed with pocket
    listening OFF — a walk where the battery matters more than the pre-roll. The
    cheaper version of that is a foreground service that holds nothing but the
    process, which needs no recorder refactor and no undocumented allowlist; it
    costs one more permanent notification, which this project has already
    decided is an acceptable price for an honest one.

    THAT CHEAPER VERSION WAS COSTED WRONG, and the correction is the point of
    this paragraph. It was argued for on the grounds that `specialUse` is the
    honest service type and carries no review risk *because this app is
    sideloaded and will never be listed*. Ben has since said the Play Store is
    possible. That premise is gone, and with it the argument: `specialUse` is
    the type Play reviews hardest, and "keeps my process alive so a button
    works" is close to the definition of what foreground service types exist to
    prevent. Assume it would be refused.

    Which leaves a better answer anyway. The only truthful type for this app is
    `microphone`, and it is truthful exactly when the microphone is open — which
    is pocket listening, which already exists and already keeps the process
    alive. So the trigger surviving a reap is not a missing service; it is a
    consequence of pocket listening being on, and the Settings copy already says
    so. Nothing to build.


[ ] The Play Store is possible — what that changes, decided in advance

    Said on 2026-07-30, and worth writing down immediately because it silently
    invalidates reasoning elsewhere. Several decisions in this repo are argued
    from "nothing is public" and "one user, one fleet", and those arguments stop
    working the moment listing is on the table. The entry above is already one
    casualty; assume there are others and check the premise before reusing any
    argument that leans on being sideloaded.

    Nothing here is urgent. The point is that some of it is cheap now and
    expensive later, and knowing which is which is the whole value of writing it
    down before anybody starts.

    CHEAP NOW:

      - `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` is in the merged manifest and nothing in `src/`
        uses it — the only "overlay" matches are style names. It arrives from a
        dependency. Draw-over-other-apps is among the most heavily scrutinised
        permissions there is, and carrying one we never call is a review
        conversation for nothing. Confirm the source and strip it.
      - Every foreground service type now needs its own Play declaration. We
        ship three: `microphone` (ours, defensible and genuinely needed),
        `location`, and `mediaPlayback` — the last from expo-audio's
        AudioControlsService, which we do not control and may not want.
      - `usesCleartextTraffic="true"`. Fine for a home network, a flag in a
        listing, and already on borrowed time.

    EXPENSIVE LATER, AND THE REAL ONES:

      - A background microphone is the single most scrutinised thing an Android
        app can do. Pocket listening needs a prominent in-app disclosure before
        the mic opens, not only the notification while it is open, and the
        listing has to explain it. The current design is honest and is most of
        the way there, which is worth knowing — the notification that "cannot be
        hidden" was argued for on ethics and turns out to be the compliant
        shape too.
      - Enrollment is one shared secret and there is no TLS. That is a
        reasonable prototype answer for one fleet on a home network and is not
        one for strangers. It is already flagged elsewhere as a design question;
        listing turns it into a blocker.
      - The Data Safety form needs an answer for location and audio: what is
        collected, whether it leaves the device, and whether it is linked to a
        person. Captures carry coordinates and the contributor identity, so the
        honest answers are "yes, yes and yes" — and `locationPrecision` and
        `censorCoords` exist precisely so the recordist decides that. Those two
        settings become a compliance story rather than only a courtesy.

    DECIDE BEFORE BUILDING ANYTHING FOR IT: whether a listed Cawtalker is the
    same app or a different one. A field tool for a handful of recordists who
    know each other and a public app that strangers install have different
    answers about identity, enrollment and what a capture may carry, and trying
    to be both is how the honest version gets watered down.

[ ] Playback is the way past the ceiling 0016 hit, and crowgpt is half of a rig

    0016 could not answer whether the crows sounded different at the stranger,
    and the reason was not sample size in the ordinary sense: "stranger" and
    "that one walk on 2026-07-31" were literally the same variable. No amount of
    observational recording fixes that, because the recordist does not control
    when a stranger appears or how far away they are.

    Suzuki's tit work is the model for the escape, and it is worth reading for
    the method rather than the findings. The compositional-syntax result works by
    recombining recorded call elements and playing them back in orders that never
    occurred naturally — ABC-D produces scan-then-approach, the reversed D-ABC
    does not, and the difference is only visible because the experimenter chose
    the order. The referential-call work presents a model predator rather than
    waiting for a real one. Both replace "record what happens" with "present a
    stimulus, repeatedly, counterbalanced".

    Applied here the cheapest version needs no new code: the same person walks
    the same transect at the same distance, many times, on many days. Repetition
    of a controlled walk is what turns n=1 into n=many, and it is the difference
    between the two recordings 0016 compared and a design that could have
    answered the question.

    The more interesting version is where crowgpt stops being a demo. A call
    generator's highest-value use is not showing that synthesis works — it is
    manufacturing playback stimuli, including recombinations and interpolations
    that no bird produced, which is precisely the manipulation the syntax result
    turns on. Nothing else in this project produces a stimulus that can be varied
    along one axis while everything else is held.

    Not started, and there is an ethics gate before it is. Playback is an active
    intervention on wild animals rather than passive recording: alarm playback
    costs birds foraging time and can pull them toward a threat that is not
    there. A neighbourhood family being studied for years is exactly the group
    that should not be repeatedly alarmed for a null result. Decide the frequency
    ceiling and the no-alarm-near-fledglings rule before the first playback, not
    after — and write down that a habituated family is a ruined study as well as
    a wronged one.


[~] Voxaboxen and BirdAVES — the backbone half is ANSWERED, the detector half is not

    Experiment 0025 ran the backbone half and it came out differently than this
    entry expected, in two ways worth keeping.

    **BirdAVES was the wrong candidate.** The 2026 comparative review (arXiv
    2508.01277) puts AVES at 63.8 and BirdAVES at 65.6 BEANS linear-probe AUROC,
    against Perch 2.0 and BEATs at 85.6. Self-supervised bird models have not
    caught the supervised ones on linear probing, which is exactly how this
    project uses an embedding. **Perch 2.0** (arXiv 2508.04665) is the candidate
    — and its own paper validates on the Weldy *call-type* dataset, so call-type
    recognition is something it was selected for rather than a side effect.

    **It wins, and the win is on the fine distinctions.** On 2,339 crow-tools
    segments, recording-grouped: macro-F1 0.849 -> 0.871, and `mob` vs `alert`
    within recordings holding both 0.637 -> 0.741, spreads separate. Perch given
    ONE second of audio beats BirdNET given three (0.856 / 0.759), which kills
    the "it just had more context" objection — and Perch at 5 s is the *worst*
    arm in the table, so 3 s is close to right for both models. Concatenating the
    two is worse than Perch alone: replace, do not add.

    **The detector half is now the better-supported half.** The entire Perch gain
    is on single-crow segments (0.848 -> 0.875). At crowCount>=2 nothing helps
    and Perch is slightly worse (0.775 -> 0.747). That is 0016's segmentation
    ceiling seen from the other side: a better backbone does not separate two
    crows calling over each other, and **Voxaboxen** — per-event onset and
    duration, built for overlapping vocalizations — remains untried and is where
    the next real gain is.

    Not started: the migration itself. It is a genuine cost — five shipped heads
    retrained, a second venv and a subprocess in the inference path (birdnetlib
    sets CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="" at import, so the two can never share a
    process), and analyzer/birdnet.py restructured to embed regions after
    grouping rather than before. analyzer/birdnet.py now refuses to load a head
    whose feature width or backbone disagrees with the pooling definition, so
    that work will fail loudly rather than at the 400th capture.

    WHAT THE DETECTOR HALF IS, carried over from the original form of this entry
    because 0025 left its case untouched and strengthened it. **Voxaboxen** is
    sound event detection with per-event onset and duration, built for
    OVERLAPPING vocalizations — the exact failure mode here. Earth Species
    open-sourced it out of the same collaboration as the León carrion-crow work
    (bioRxiv, April 2026, Cusimano/Hoffman/Guzón-García with Canestrari and
    Baglione), where it also classified caller class (tagged adult / other adult
    / chick / cuckoo nestling), a harder version of a question this corpus
    cannot currently ask at all.

    Why it is the ceiling: 0016 lost 49% of its detected call units to 9 dB of
    added noise, and the segmenter it used cannot separate overlapping birds at
    all — it finds bursts of band energy, so five crows calling over each other
    is one unit or none. That bounds every rate and duration measure this
    project makes.

    BE CLEAR ABOUT WHAT IT WOULD NOT FIX. A better detector removes the
    segmentation ceiling; it does not remove the range confound, because a
    distant quiet caw is missing from the audio rather than missed by the
    detector. 0016's degradation control stays necessary either way.

    Evaluate before the next head is trained, not after.


[ ] Our call types are all semantic; León's mostly are not, and that is the finding

    The 2026 León/Earth Species repertoire paper is easy to dismiss on its
    taxonomy — squawk and grunt do not describe what American crows do. But the
    paper's own position is that its names are mostly *not* semantic, and that is
    the part that transfers.

    They split 40 call types into three super-categories. Only "exceptional" (22
    types) got hand-made categories, and those were rare. Caws and grunts —
    **72% of all vocalizations** — were treated as graded continua and cut by
    duration and amplitude quantiles instead: caw0-caw5 (0 shortest, 5 longest),
    grunt0-5 with Q and L for quiet and loud. No semantic names at all. And it
    worked: shorter caws and shorter grunts both indicated the signaller was
    flying.

    Our vocabulary is semantic all the way down — alarm call, scold, rattle,
    contact call, flight call. If the same gradedness holds for American crows,
    then `alarm call` is a continuum being forced into one bin, and 0016 already
    measured the axis without recognising it: median unit duration 0.224 s on one
    recording against 0.128 s on another, which was read there as a confound to
    be explained away.

    Cheap first test, no new collection needed: split the 25 alarm-call spans by
    duration quantile and ask whether the bins associate with different contexts.
    Thin, and the range confound from 0016 applies to amplitude but much less to
    duration. If a split shows anything at all it justifies a graded axis beside
    the call type rather than more names.

    Second finding, and it indicts the whole recording setup rather than the
    labels: **59% of their vocalizations were quieter than chick sounds** picked
    up on the same tag. Their words — "the crows' widely-known caws were some of
    their loudest vocalizations, and so give a skewed view of their communication
    system." A handheld mic at 5-20 m records the loud tail of the distribution.
    That is why this corpus is 55% alarm call, and it means the close-range calls
    that coordinate chick care are not merely under-sampled here, they are
    inaudible. Nothing on a sidewalk fixes it; only a closer microphone does.

    Third, smaller, but a schema gap: their main social variable is whether the
    signaller was a breeding male, breeding female, or helper, and the only six
    social-category-specific calls were exceptional ones. We record what the
    *recordist* is to the crows and nothing about which bird called. The Poe
    family has exactly that structure — Edgar is a yearling helper — so the
    question is askable here and currently unrecordable.

CROWGPT: THE ROBOT IS THE THREE CODEBOOKS THAT LEARNED NOTHING

    0019 measured the model's held-out predictive power stage by stage and found
    it decays to nothing. Bits recovered over the codec's own marginal go 1.96,
    1.32, 1.01, 0.54, 0.21, then negative. Stages 5, 6 and 7 predict no better
    than drawing from the marginal, so three of eight codebooks are generated at
    essentially chance — a decent coarse signal with structured-looking noise
    laid over it. That is the most concrete mechanical account of "cyborg" the
    project has, and it is not reachable by any sampling parameter, because the
    model is correctly calibrated about its own uncertainty at every stage.

    So the lever is capacity or the objective, exactly where 0017 said it would
    end up ("the codec is not the lever"). Options, none costed yet:

      - Per-stage output heads. Adds parameters where the loss actually is,
        rather than taxing a shared trunk. The flat cross-entropy currently
        weights all eight codebooks equally while only the first three carry
        learned signal.
      - Fix the underfitting first, because it may be the whole story. dropout
        sits at 0.25 on a model that cannot fit its own training batches, the
        cosine floors at 10% of peak and never anneals, and the batch is
        silently clipped from 32 to 16 by a memory budget nobody chose. All
        three are free to change. See the entry below.
      - Accept eight codebooks are more than this data supports and ask whether
        five carry the caw. 0014 says lower bitrate loses it, but 0014 tested
        BITRATE, not truncation of a model trained at 6 kbps.

    DO NOT chase per-stage temperature (0019 killed it, twice over), the codec
    (0014 and 0017), or the codec frame grid (0020 measured the model adding
    nothing to it beyond what the tokenizer already puts there).

CROWGPT: CALL-TYPE CONDITIONING IS DATA-BOUND NOW, AND THE FIX IS HUMAN LABELS

        alarm call  26,876 bouts  92.9%      song  1,113 bouts  16.4%

[ ] Merging crowgpt with a future "crow translate" — the merge is free, and it is
    not the part that makes it a translator

    Asked 2026-07-31: can crowgpt (human -> crow) and a future semantics model
    (crow -> human) be joined into a round-trip translator once there are enough
    labels? Written down now because the answer changes what to collect, and the
    interface it would run through is cheap to fix today and expensive later.

    THE MERGE ITSELF IS ALREADY DONE, in a degenerate form. The analyzer maps
    audio to a CORE class and crowgpt takes a CORE class as a prefix token, so
    the loop closes today with nothing built. What comes out is a four-symbol
    phrasebook run in both directions. Whatever "complete translator" means, the
    plumbing is not the missing piece and no engineering effort should be spent
    on it.

    THE VOCABULARIES DO NOT MATCH, AND THAT IS THE ONE CHEAP FIX. crowgpt's
    CORE is `("alarm call", "begging", "call", "song")` — four — because it is
    derived from crow-tools' flags via FLAG_TO_CORE, which has no flight-call
    flag. `config_store.CORE_CLASSES` is five. So **flight call, the class with
    the best AUC in the whole project (0.91), is exactly the one that cannot be
    requested from the generator.** Nobody chose that; it fell out of which
    corpus each side was built from. And the mob/alert split filed above would
    move the interface again. Reconcile the two lists before anything is built
    across them, not after.

    THE INTENDED DESIGN IS NOT A THREE-STAGE PIPE, and an earlier draft of this
    entry got it wrong. Ben's target (2026-07-31) is to condition the generator
    on **context directly** — say "food here" and get a call matching the
    food-tagged calls in the field corpus — with the call-type token understood
    as the prototype that proved the mechanism rather than the interface. That is
    the better design and it dissolves the objection the earlier draft raised.
    There is no lookup table in the middle and no need for one: the axis is a
    prefix token like any other, and 0021 already showed prefix conditioning
    works and scales monotonically with data behind each value.

    It does mean the conditioning set can only ever come from the FIELD corpus.
    The call-type axis was free because a head could pseudo-label 57,000 archive
    bouts; an archive clip carries no witnessed context and never will. So
    every context value is paid for one field session at a time. That is the
    whole cost structure of this plan, and it is worth being precise about
    because it is much better for the food case than for any other.

    THE 6 FOOD SPANS DO NOT AGREE ON A CALL TYPE, which is the finding that
    should change what gets collected. Every `human_gives_food` event is attached
    to a label, across 5 captures on 5 separate days — good grouping — but the
    call types are:

        cap 69  call        cap 104 begging       cap 100 alarm call
        cap 75  call        cap 104 begging       cap 113 alarm call

    Three CORE classes over six spans, 81 seconds in total. So "my food-here
    calls" is not currently a coherent acoustic class in the corpus, and the
    reason is a vocabulary one: **`human_gives_food` records what the human did,
    not what the crow said.** Food being present while a fledgling begs and food
    being present while something else gets alarmed at are both in there.

    Nothing in the vocabulary can currently say "the crow announced food". By
    this file's own rule — context is what a human witnessed, a label is what the
    bird said — a recruitment/food call is a LABEL, not a context tag, and
    SEED_LABELS already takes new words that map down to a core. That addition is
    the cheapest high-value change available here: server-side, no migration, no
    wire bump, the vocabulary reaches the phone through GET /api/config. Do it
    before the collection ramps, not after, or the sessions produce more of the
    same ambiguity.

    THE EXPORT THROWS CONTEXT AWAY TODAY. `pipelines/ingest/export_manifest.py`
    emits `{"path": ..., "type": <core classes>}` and nothing else — no context
    key, no span. The path from field labels to a trainable set drops the exact
    variable this plan turns on. Also cheap, also worth doing before collecting
    rather than after, because otherwise the sessions land in a pipe that
    discards them.

    ONLY PLAYBACK SEPARATES THOSE, which joins this entry to "Playback is the way
    past the ceiling 0016 hit, and crowgpt is half of a rig" above. A playback rig
    is the only test where the crows grade the output, and it validates BOTH legs
    at once: whether a generated alarm functions as an alarm is also evidence
    about what the label meant. The ethics gate in that entry applies unchanged
    and comes first.

    THE GAP IS 74x, NOT THE THREE ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE IT LOOKS LIKE. 0021's rule
    is a couple of thousand bouts per conditioning value; at 3 s a bout that is
    about 100 minutes of food-context call audio. The corpus holds 81 seconds. A
    factor of ~74 is a lot and it is not hopeless in the way 1000x would be.

    AND FOOD IS THE ONE CONTEXT BEN CONTROLS. This is the reason to be optimistic
    about this axis specifically and pessimistic about the others. Every other
    key in the 26-tag vocabulary waits on the world to supply it — a hawk, a dog,
    a stranger — which is precisely why `dog_visible` has 1 event and
    `stranger_approaching` has 1. Feeding is an intervention that can be
    scheduled, repeated and counterbalanced. Of all 26 values, "food here" is the
    most collectable rather than the least, and 20 scheduled feeding sessions is
    a month of ordinary effort rather than a research programme.

    That is the Suzuki method from the playback entry applied to COLLECTION
    instead of playback, and it should be recognised as the same move: stop
    recording what happens and start presenting a situation on purpose.

    THE CONFOUND SURVIVES ANYWAY. Every food event is Ben with peanuts, so a
    generator conditioned on it learns a scene that contains him, his mic, his
    street and his time of day, and cannot separate "the food call" from "the
    call made when Ben shows up". Five separate days is decent grouping and does
    not fix it. There is also a domain gap nobody has measured: crowgpt was
    trained on the archive corpus through EnCodec at 24 kHz, and this conditioning data
    is a phone and a Rode on a sidewalk.

    EVALUATION IS THE BINDING CONSTRAINT, EXACTLY AS IN 0021. That experiment's
    discipline was that a judge which cannot classify real bouts says nothing
    about generated ones — and a context judge trained on 6 examples is worth
    nothing. But the blinded listening rig generalises for free and needs no
    model: forced choice, a generated "food here" against a real food-tagged
    span, the same 60-trial sheet and the same pooled report. Use it rather than
    inventing a second yardstick.

    WHAT IT WOULD ACTUALLY DO TODAY, stated plainly so nobody discovers it later:
    alarm call round-trips (92.9% conditioning recall), begging half does (50%),
    song and `call` do not (16.4%, 3.8%) — and blinded listening says the audio
    is identifiable as generated three times in four. A demo of the round trip
    would be a demo of the alarm call and nothing else.

    THE DEEPER THREAT IS THE León RESULT one entry down: 72% of vocalizations are
    graded continua cut by duration and amplitude, not semantic categories. If
    that holds for American crows, a discrete class token is the wrong pipe for
    both directions, and building a round trip on top of it hardens the wrong
    interface. The cheap first test is already written there — split the alarm
    spans by duration quantile and ask whether the bins associate with different
    contexts. Do that before the round trip, not after.

    THE FOOD CALL IS THE ONE PLAYBACK THAT COSTS THE BIRDS NOTHING. The playback
    entry above gates alarm playback for good reasons — it costs foraging time
    and pulls birds toward a threat that is not there. A synthetic food call
    played *while food is actually put down* is not deception at all; it is the
    same contingency these crows already have with Ben, and it makes the round
    trip testable without the ethics problem. If any part of this gets built
    first, it should be this one, because it is the only version where the crows
    grade the output and nobody is worse off for it.

    SO THE ORDER, and it is mostly not model work:
      1. Add a food/recruitment LABEL so the corpus can say what the bird said
         rather than only what the human did. Free, server-side.
      2. Carry context through export_manifest. Cheap, and blocks everything
         downstream until it exists.
      3. Reconcile the two CORE lists (four vs five) while nothing depends on
         them yet.
      4. Schedule feeding sessions and label them. This is the actual work and
         it is the only step that takes months.
      5. Run the graded-axis test from the León entry before hardening a
         discrete context token, in case the axis wants to be continuous.
      6. Then condition, and judge it on the existing blinded sheet.
      7. Then playback, with food, as the real validation.

    The merge itself is still the last line of code and the least of it.


CROWGPT: THE PRETRAINED ROUTE WAS TRIED, AND IT LOST TO THE 18M FROM SCRATCH

A BUTTON IN THE APP THAT GENERATES A CROW CALL

    Ben's, 2026-07-31. Press a button, pick a call type, hear a synthesised caw.
    The shape is clear and the plumbing is mostly a copy of something that
    already works — a generation worker alongside `fieldserver/worker/`'s
    analyze_worker, polling a `generation_jobs` table, calling into crowgpt the
    way analyze_worker calls into the analyzer. 156 lines is the whole precedent.

    THE ARCHITECTURE HAS ONE REAL OBSTACLE AND IT IS NOT THE MODEL. Sync is
    push-only: the app never pulls from the server, and that rule is what stops
    a stale server copy overwriting a field recording. A generate button needs
    audio to travel server -> phone, which is the direction that does not exist.
    Restore is the single exception and it earned that by being unable to
    contradict anything — it only fetches captures the phone does not have.

    Generated audio has the same property, so the honest framing is that this is
    a SECOND exception of the same kind rather than a hole in the rule: the
    phone has no truth about a clip the server just invented. Worth writing the
    justification down in the same terms Restore's was, and worth deciding
    whether generated clips land somewhere separate from `captures` entirely —
    they are not recordings, nothing was observed, and a synthesised caw sitting
    in the capture list is a corpus contaminant waiting to be counted. The ★
    Keep flag exists because that distinction was already hard once.

    IT IS A JOB, NOT A REQUEST. crowgpt has no KV cache, so a bout costs roughly
    five seconds on the 4070 and generation is quadratic in length. Queue it,
    poll it, notify — exactly the analysis flow. Do not put it behind a
    synchronous endpoint that holds a phone waiting.

    ALSO NEEDS: a WIRE_VERSION bump in the commit that adds the fields, since
    this is a genuine API change; a decision about WHICH checkpoint ships, which
    0023 and the queued LoRA runs will inform; and a story for the field server
    being on a laptop without the GPU, because crowgpt's venv carries torch and
    the analyzer's deliberately does not.

    THE PART THAT NEEDS A DECISION BEFORE ANY CODE. If this button gets pressed
    outdoors, it stops being a demo and becomes a PLAYBACK EXPERIMENT. Broadcast
    an alarm call near the Poe Family and the birds respond — that is the whole
    reason alarm calls exist — so it changes their behaviour, it can distress
    them, and any recording made afterwards is of birds reacting to us rather
    than to their world. It would also quietly poison the corpus: a mobbing
    response provoked by a phone is indistinguishable, in the capture list, from
    one provoked by a hawk.

    SETTLED, 2026-07-31: Ben's answer is that it is not for outdoor use. This is
    a demo — something to play to a person to show the project is real — and not
    a field instrument. That decides the design rather than merely constraining
    it. The safeguard does not need to be elaborate: refuse or loudly warn when
    a listening session is open, since the microphone being live is the case
    where generated audio can reach both a bird and the corpus. No playback
    protocol, no exclusion flag on nearby captures, none of the apparatus a real
    playback study would need.

    Which also means the bar is different. A demo is judged on whether the clip
    sounds convincing to a HUMAN standing next to you, so it should ship the
    checkpoint that wins the listening test rather than the one with the best
    BirdNET rate — 0022 having just shown that those are not the same thing, and
    that the BirdNET number is circular in crowgpt's favour. Generated clips
    still do not belong in `captures` for the reason above.

    Playback as a real method stays available and stays out of scope. It is
    arguably the most interesting thing this project could eventually do, and a
    controlled study is a different project with its own protocol. The point of
    writing this down is so the demo button cannot quietly become one.


EXPERIMENT: DO ALARM CALLS HAVE SEQUENTIAL STRUCTURE? DO UNITS CLUSTER AT ALL?

    Ben's, 2026-08-01. Two questions stacked, and the second has to be answered
    first: can individual call units be grouped into a repeatable alphabet, and
    if so, does the ORDER of units within an alarm bout carry structure beyond
    what their frequencies alone would produce.

    MOST OF THE MACHINERY EXISTS. segment.py already finds units — energy bursts
    in the corvid band, UNIT_MIN_SEC 0.06, UNIT_GAP_SEC 0.08, validated at 12 dB
    by 0013 — and a bout holds a median of about 3.8 of them. BirdNET embeddings
    give a vector per unit. 0021 makes alarm the only class with enough data to
    try this on: 26,876 bouts, and the head's alarm recall is the one number in
    that experiment that is properly validated (n=127). So the pipeline is
    roughly segment -> embed -> cluster -> count transitions.

    THE TRAP, AND THIS PROJECT HAS ALREADY WALKED INTO IT ONCE. 0017 clustered
    crow audio and found that five crows cluster exactly as much as one crow
    does — the structure it recovered was recording identity and position, not
    bird identity. Every unit inside a bout shares one recording, one
    microphone, one distance and one background. So a naive clustering will put
    all the units of a bout in the same cluster for channel reasons, and the
    transition table will look gloriously structured while measuring nothing but
    "these came from the same file". Any version of this that does not control
    for that is measuring the recorder.

    Minimum defences: require clusters to be reproducible ACROSS recordings
    before using them, hold recordings out grouped exactly as 0018 does, and
    check whether cluster identity predicts recording identity — if it does, the
    alphabet is a channel fingerprint and the experiment stops there.

    THE BASELINE IS A SHUFFLE. Compare real bouts against their own units
    reordered at random. That holds composition fixed and destroys only the
    order, so any excess predictability is sequential structure rather than a
    fact about which units are common. Compare alarm bouts against non-alarm
    ones too — structure that appears everywhere is a fact about crows, not
    about alarm.

    THE BOUT CAP IS A PROBLEM HERE AND IS NOT ELSEWHERE. segment.py merges
    bursts within 1.5 s and caps a bout at 3.0 seconds. That cap was chosen for
    the generator, where it keeps sequences trainable, and it is exactly wrong
    for this question: a long alarm sequence gets cut at three seconds, which
    truncates the structure being looked for. Re-segment with a much larger cap
    for this experiment rather than reusing data/crowgpt/bouts as it stands.

    DO NOT CALL IT SYNTAX ON THIS EVIDENCE. Sequential dependency is what this
    can measure. Syntax in the sense the literature means — order changing
    MEANING, compositional structure — requires showing the birds respond
    differently to reordered sequences, and that is a playback experiment, which
    the entry above just put out of scope. Worth being precise about, because
    "crows have syntax" is exactly the sentence that would get repeated without
    its caveat. Compositional syntax has been demonstrated in other birds (the
    Japanese tit work is the usual reference and is worth reading before
    designing this), so the question is a real one and the bar is known.

    WORTH DOING WHEN there is a reason to prefer it over the labelling work
    0021 left behind. It needs no new recordings and no human labelling, which
    makes it unusually cheap for a question this interesting, and a measured
    null — units do not cluster, or order carries nothing — is a genuinely
    useful answer that nobody has written down for this corpus.


[ ] Should the sticky observer role follow *place*?

    Left open when the sticky default shipped on 2026-08-01. "They know me as"
    and the distance band now carry the last answer forward to the next capture,
    which is one value everywhere — right most of the time on Ben's own block
    and wrong the moment he records in a park across town.

    THE ARGUMENT FOR WAITING, and it is why this is here rather than in TODO. A
    default that is right 90% of the time is more dangerous than one that is
    right 60%: nobody checks the former. A location-aware version would raise
    the hit rate into exactly the band where the field stops being read — and
    observer_role is the variable that flips the meaning of everything else on
    the tape, so a wrong one that goes unnoticed is worse than no default at
    all.

    So the thing to watch is not "would this be more accurate". It is whether a
    capture ever turns up carrying a remembered role that was wrong for where it
    was recorded. One of those is the evidence that decides this, and until then
    the simple version is the honest one.

    Shape if it is ever wanted: the remembered value keyed by region (captures
    already carry one) rather than a single string, falling back to the last
    answer anywhere when the region is new. Cheap to build on what exists —
    `stickyCaptureDefaults` in domain/settings.ts is the one place that would
    change, and it is already pure and tested.

    Deliberately NOT applied to the group counts, which are genuinely per
    sighting: carrying "3 adults" forward would invent an observation.


[ ] Learn what a flight call actually sounds like, so it can be hand-labelled

    The one CORE class the corpus cannot check the model on, and the gap is
    structural rather than a matter of volume.

    **crow-tools has ZERO flight-call examples.** There is no flight-call flag
    upstream, so all 40 positives in the American Crow head come from
    the archive set — whole-clip recordings, other continents, pooled a different way
    (std block non-zero, where crow-tools' is exactly zero). Experiment 0025's
    preflight found the two corpora are trivially separable on that block alone,
    with every flight-call positive on one side of it. So the head's entire
    notion of "flight call" is learned from audio that looks nothing like a
    phone in a Salt Lake City park, and its shipped 0.975 AUC is measured across
    a boundary that predicts the label.

    It shows up in the field, not just in a metric. On kept captures the head
    guesses flight call exactly twice and both disagree with Ben's labels:
    capture 100 @42s (he said alarm call, head said flight call 0.685) and
    capture 116 @12s (he said begging, head said flight call 0.916).

    **But 116 may not be an error, and that is the interesting part.** His
    observation reads "Baby caws north of me. Family of 4 fly down south into a
    tree. Baby cries and other crow caws." Five birds. The flight call and the
    begging can be different individuals, and the head reported all three —
    flight call 0.916, begging 0.821, call 0.732 — in one 15-second span. A
    single `begging` label cannot cover a span with five birds in it, so "the
    head is wrong" and "the head heard a bird the label could not hold" are both
    consistent with what is stored. Nothing in the corpus distinguishes them,
    because nothing records which bird called. See the caller-field entry.

    There is also a real chance the category is graded rather than crisp. Work
    on hand-raised corvids (Emu, Little Raven and White-winged Chough) found
    begging calls are developmental precursors for learned vocalizations, with
    pulse duration shifting toward the other call types the same juveniles give.
    A fledgling cry partway to a flight call may be exactly that — partway —
    which is the same gradedness the León paper found in adult caws and grunts,
    and which our semantic vocabulary has no way to express.

    WHAT TO DO, in order of cost:

      - Listen to reference flight calls until they are recognisable by ear.
        The corpus's American Crow `flight call` recordings are already in
        data/species/american-crow/.
      - Then hand-label them in the field corpus, including the negative case:
        marking a suspected flight call as `begging` is worth as much, and is
        the only thing that can tell the two apart in this acoustic setting.
      - Until there are some, treat every flight-call prediction on field audio
        as unverified. Worth badging in the app rather than leaving to memory —
        it is the one class whose training data comes from somewhere else.

    Ten or so labelled either way across four recordings would let 0025's probe
    say whether flight call and begging are separable on a phone at all. If they
    are not, the honest move is to merge them for juveniles rather than keep a
    class the corpus cannot support.

[ ] Wired and USB headphones get a vibration where AirPods get the words

    `CuePlayer.bluetoothOutputConnected` decides between speaking and buzzing,
    and only counts Bluetooth outputs — wired or USB headphones are treated as
    nothing-connected. Correct for the Rode case (the USB port is taken by the
    microphone) and wrong in general. Left alone twice now, 2026-08-01 both
    times, because the heartbeat shares the method and each cue fix was
    already touching enough. Revisit if anyone ever records with wired
    earbuds; until then the asymmetry costs nothing.


[x] WP2 (coarse generator + fine-stage extender) — superseded by the decoder
    finetune, decided not to, 2026-08-10

    The planned stage 2 after the corpus work. Its whole selling point was
    raising the ceiling at fixed learnability; 0063 did that with zero model
    change — same K=8 tokens, finetuned decoder, test FAD 34.2 against a
    30.9 raw-vs-raw floor, Ben's ear at "no more than 10%". The texture
    defect the extender's fine stages were meant to supply is functionally
    closed, the ~3 FAD of remaining headroom does not buy a tokenizer
    migration plus two models, and the one extender actually built scored
    0/24 in a sitting (see 0050's README). The defects still standing after
    0064 — pulse density, bout structure — are coarse-stage and model-side,
    which an extender does not touch. Revisit only if a future defect is
    shown to live in the fine stages of a codec the decoder finetune cannot
    reach.

[ ] If nothing is transparent, the tokenizer question reopens as a codec
    question — and DAC should not have been dropped

    0028 measured the EnCodec codebook ladder and it plateaus: spectral
    distance to raw falls 1.165 -> 1.035 from K=8 to K=32 and flattens by
    K=24, against 3.090 for two unrelated real bouts. At the maximum bandwidth
    EnCodec offers, the round-trip still sits a third of the way to a
    stranger's recording. More codebooks is not the lever.

    0017 rejected DAC on retention AT MATCHED TOKEN COUNT — 0.483 at 1,813
    tokens against EnCodec's 0.973 at 1,800. That constraint was load-bearing
    in the rejection, and it is exactly what PLAN-TIMBRE's WP2 argues is no
    longer binding: if fine stages can be filled in by a cheap second model,
    the sequence-length budget is not what it was in July. DAC 44 kHz at 9
    codebooks also won on spectral distance (1.013 vs 1.151) while losing
    slightly on retention.

    So the question to revisit is not "is DAC better" — 0017 answered that for
    its own constraint — but "is DAC better once tokens are affordable". A DAC
    rung belongs in the next version of 0028's ladder; dac_codec.py already
    exists and the page builder takes arbitrary arms.

    REVISIT WHEN: the blinded rate-codec-blind.html sitting comes back saying
    K=16 is not distinguishable from K=8. That is the result that says the
    residual tail is not where the scratch lives.

[ ] A 10-point equivalence gate needs ~200 trials per arm and nobody has
    budgeted for that

    PLAN-TIMBRE's gates are written as "good-rate within 10 points of raw" at
    15 trials per arm. At n=15 the standard error on a proportion is about 13
    points, so the test cannot resolve its own margin — and because it is an
    EQUIVALENCE gate, failing to resolve means declaring transparency that was
    never demonstrated. The bias runs toward passing.

    Sizing, at p≈0.5: +/-10 points at 95% needs ~96 per arm for a single
    proportion and ~200 per arm for a difference between two. The project's
    standing sitting is 60 trials total. So a real equivalence claim is four
    sittings, and Ben's ears are the budget PLAN-TIMBRE says to spend at gates
    and not on iteration.

    The consequence worth watching: several results in this project are
    phrased as "could not tell", which is an equivalence claim collected at
    difference-test power. 0028's page says so on its own facts panel rather
    than leaving it implied, and anything quoting a null by ear should do the
    same.


[~] Video or dictation? Mostly answered 2026-08-02 — film, but do not watch it

    The question was which to prioritise going forward. Two of the three
    premises turned out to be already settled in the tree, and the third is the
    real one.

    THE MICROPHONE WORRY IS UNFOUNDED. `VideoCaptureScreen` runs the camera
    MUTED on purpose and records sound through the same `raw-audio` path an
    audio-only capture uses — its docstring says the camera's own track "would
    be compressed and speech-processed — fine to watch, useless to train on —
    and it would also take the microphone, which raw-audio needs for the 48 kHz
    signal the model actually learns from". Two files, not one muxed mp4, so a
    sync over a bad link delivers the audio first. `preRollSec` is carried
    through, so the sound still reaches back fifteen seconds before the picture
    starts. Filming costs nothing in audio quality.

    And the mic is cardioid and mounted on the phone, so framing a crow AIMS it
    at the crow — the directionality 0001 and 0004 said matters most is enforced
    by filming rather than lost to it. Worth remembering that 0009 never
    established the Rode beats the built-in anyway: 2.5 dB worse in band SNR
    against the better built-in capture, 6.8 dB better against the weaker, n=1
    against n=2.

    THE ARGUMENT FOR VIDEO IS CROW COUNT, AND IT IS SPECIFIC. 0025 found the
    ceiling is overlapping callers: macro-F1 0.848 -> 0.875 at crowCount==1, and
    at crowCount>=2 nothing helps (0.775 -> 0.750, Perch slightly worse). Crow
    count gates every backbone improvement available — and it currently comes
    from "how many crows the reviewer heard", which is audio review, which is
    exactly what fails when birds call over each other. Video breaks that
    circularity. Nothing else on offer does.

    THE REAL OBJECTION IS ATTENTION, AND THE CODEBASE AGREES WITH IT.
    `domain/context.ts`: "Tagged in the moment, because it is the one thing that
    genuinely cannot be recovered later. You can re-listen to a recording a year
    from now and still label the call; you cannot go back and see whether there
    was a dog." Context is DEFINED as the irrecoverable half that depends on a
    human witnessing it, so attention in the moment is the scarce resource the
    whole matrix runs on.

    The asymmetry that makes it worse: what a recordist misses while framing a
    shot is disproportionately what is OUTSIDE the frame — the hawk behind them,
    the second crow in another tree — which the video cannot recover either. A
    pure loss, not a transfer. It also makes the two modes non-independent:
    filming can thin the dictation channel by reducing what there is to dictate.

    THE RESOLUTION: the cost belongs to WATCHING THE VIEWFINDER, not to
    recording. Start it, prop or mount the phone at the usual perch, and watch
    the birds directly. The Poe Family is habituated at a known location, which
    is the case where a fixed frame works — no unpredictable subject to track.
    That yields the Rode on the pristine audio path, video for the crow count,
    and full attention for the tagging nothing else can produce. Residual cost is
    real and bounded: a fixed frame misses birds that leave it.

    Dictation keeps its own job — what was noticed but could not be filmed —
    and keeps its own cost: `dictationSession.ts` shows one microphone session
    means a dictation closes the rolling buffer and drops the ring, so narrating
    costs the held past of the thing being narrated.

    STILL OPEN, and both are cheap:

      - How often are the birds actually in shot? Nobody has counted. A visual
        channel that is empty most of the time recovers less than it promises.
      - Is the attention cost real in Ben's own data? Compare context-chip counts
        and dictation length on sessions where he filmed against ones where he
        did not. If tagging is measurably thinner on video days, the effect is
        showing up in records that already exist — no new study needed.

    REVISIT WHEN: a few mounted-camera sessions have happened and there is
    enough to count both of the above.

THE LISTENING PAGES DO NOT PLAY IN FIREFOX

    ABXPage plays in Chrome and is silent in Firefox as of 2026-08-03, after
    two real bugs were found and fixed (a seek issued before the media loaded,

HOW MANY CLIPS A SITTING CAN ACTUALLY HOLD — measured, 2026-08-03

    Ben asked. The answer from this project's own thirteen saved sittings is
    that quality falls off far earlier than the sitting sizes assume.

    DECISION TIME COLLAPSES THROUGH A SITTING. First quarter against last
    quarter, median across 13 sittings: -15%. On the two long ABX sittings it
    was -35% and -48% — 5.5s a trial down to 3.6s, and 7.2s down to 3.8s.
    Accuracy does not rise to match, so this is disengagement rather than
    practice.

    ABX ACCURACY DECLINES WITH POSITION. Pooled over every ABX trial:
      Q1 0.679   Q2 0.558   Q3 0.442   Q4 0.500
    Partly confounded — the arms are not balanced across quartiles — but the
    cleanest within-arm comparison points the same way. On the 20-trial
    instrument check, an easy task, the anchor went 10/10 in the first half and
    7/10 in the second.

    WORKING GUIDANCE: about 30-40 trials for a discrimination task, 60-80 for a
    rating task, and if more power is needed run several short sittings rather
    than one long one. Every sitting needs its own within-session anchor,
    because pooling across sittings only works if each one can be shown to have
    functioned (0033's raw anchor moved and its cross-sitting comparisons had
    to be thrown out).

    The 120-trial sittings this project has standardised on are roughly three
    times too long, and the last quarter of each is probably noise.

DO NOT INTERLEAVE SITTINGS FROM DIFFERENT AGENTS

    On 2026-08-03 Ben was asked to grade by two agents at once and said "it is
    hard grading". That is a confound, not just a workload complaint: listener
    drift in this project has been measured at 8.3 points evening to morning on
    identical weights, and fatigue carried over from another agent's sixty
    clips lands inside the next agent's numbers with nothing recording that it
    happened.

    Anything comparing arms WITHIN one sitting is safe — that is the whole
    reason interleaved and same-session controls exist here. What is not safe
    is comparing across sittings, or reading a sitting that followed someone
    else's without knowing it did.

    If an agent needs ears, it should ask whether one has already been spent
    that day, and record the answer in the session note. Cheap, and it is the
    only way the confound is visible later.

[ ] A FEEDING STATION ON THE WORK ROOF — designed 2026-08-04, in
    phases/roof-station.md

    Ben's, and the design is written down rather than summarised here because it
    needs real discussion: a second site, a second family, an unattended
    recorder, a staged build with a gate on every stage, and a permission
    conversation with a building owner.

    THE REASON TO BUILD IT IS NOT THE OBVIOUS ONE, which is the part worth
    carrying in this file. "More recordings" is the weakest argument for it.
    The strong one is that it is the only way to record these birds with NO
    HUMAN IN THE RECORDING, and that goes straight at the confound already
    written down above — observer_role across every field capture is `feeder`
    (6) and `feeding_now` (7), so "a known feeder is present" is perfectly
    confounded with "a recording exists". Recruiting neighbours gets a
    DIFFERENT human, which controls familiarity and does not control presence.
    Only an unattended station does.

    The protocol follows from it: put the food down and LEAVE, immediately, and
    let the recorder run. Two minutes of `feeding_now` followed by twenty of
    nobody-there is a matched pair over the same birds at the same site through
    the same microphone, and it costs nothing but walking away.

    THREE MORE THINGS IT BUYS, in order:

      - Field negatives by the hour, from the exact mic and noise floor the
        positives come from. 0036 found the shipped head assigns a call type at
        p > 0.5 to 97.0% of segments with no crow in them, and TODO already
        says field negatives are the cheapest fix. A station produces them as a
        by-product of being left on.
      - Contact calls, which the corpus has none of — `contact_call` is in the
        vocabulary and empty, and the `call` class is a rattle detector. A
        feeding station is where arrival and food-associated calling happens.
      - A second family as a TEST set that can falsify a number, not as
        training volume. Same reasoning 0036 forced onto the mobbing labels.
      - THE ACTIVITY CLOCK (added 2026-08-20): calls-per-hour × temperature ×
        sun angle from continuous listening, with no observer in the loop.
        Ben's captures cluster 7–11 am and ~6 pm, but so does Ben — capture
        times are perfectly confounded with his work schedule, so "crows go
        quiet noon–4" is currently textbook knowledge plus one afternoon
        counter-example (capture 116, 4 pm at 37 °C), not a measurement. The
        station separates crow schedule from recordist schedule as a
        by-product, and its histogram is what the scout layer's "best window
        today" should eventually run on instead of assumptions.

    AND WHAT IT IS BAD AT, which must not be forgotten when the capture count
    starts climbing: a station has no witnessed context. It feeds the acoustic
    problem and the abstain problem and barely touches semantics. It does not
    replace handheld sessions.

    DECIDE BEFORE THE FIRST UPLOAD, not after — this is the part that will hurt
    if it is skipped, because a station makes more captures in a fortnight than
    the corpus has in its whole life:

      - a `source` on captures (handheld / station / seed / upload). ★ Keep
        cannot carry it: keep means "this is real", and station audio is real
        AND different. No wire bump — the phone never sends it, the server
        defaults phone pushes to `handheld`.
      - an `absent` observer role, "Nobody there". NULL already means "not
        recorded", and writing NULL for "demonstrably nobody" conflates unknown
        with none — the same mistake this repo has now written down for
        config_version, band_snr_db and not_a_crow-versus-none. Server-side
        seed plus a translation, no migration, no app build.
      - a segmentation step, because a four-hour block is not a capture:
        block -> analyzer/ -> corvid windows -> one capture per region.

    IT ALSO FINALLY MAKES THE WIND EXPERIMENT RUNNABLE. TODO's windshield entry
    has been stuck because no experiment ever sampled wind; a station can run
    two matched mics side by side, jammed and bare, for a whole windy day. That
    is the comparison handheld A/B could never make.

    REVISIT WHEN the permission is granted and Gate 0 passes. Both are in TODO
    because they have long lead times and everything else waits on them.


[ ] Mine long recordings for NEGATIVES — the one data intervention already
    measured to pay

    0036 is unambiguous: handed 572 segments a human marked crowCount = 0, the
    shipped four-class head assigns a call type at p > 0.5 to 97.0% of them, mean
    top probability 0.898, calling 388 of 572 "alarm call". A five-class head
    with `none` rejects 70.6% for a cost of 0.021 macro-F1. label_defs already
    carries not_a_crow with trains = 0.

    0036 framed this as a labelling job — "the cheapest possible thing for a
    human to say". It is cheaper than that. A long outdoor recording is mostly
    negatives already and no human has to say anything. Every hour pulled by any
    plan in this file produces them as a by-product.

    Measured yields, BirdNET min_conf 0.10, no location filter (n=3, and the
    spread IS the result):

      XC517764  foreground Carrion Crow, 16:57   18.0% corvid  -> 14 min neg
      XC727489  crow in `also`, UK estuary 30:00  7.3% corvid  -> 28 min neg
      XC736842  crow in `also`, DE garden  53:16  0.4% corvid  -> 53 min neg

    Foreground beats background by ~an order of magnitude and background yield is
    unstable, so `also:` is a bad basis for committing tens of GB — it means "this
    species is audible somewhere in this file", possibly once in 53 minutes. But
    for negatives the low-yield recordings are the GOOD ones, and they are the
    best negatives obtainable: same microphone, same site, same session as the
    crow, i.e. the exact acoustic background a crow calls against.

    The pool, if wanted: also:"Corvus corone" len:">900" = 594 recordings, median
    30 min, max 55 min, ~463 h, ~40-70 GB.
    analyzer/ already does "a path in, findings out" with no server, so the
    mining pipeline exists. Pilot ~20 recordings and measure the yield
    distribution before committing — n=3 with a 45x spread is not a rate.

[x] Playback of generated calls to the Poe family — DECIDED NOT TO

    It came up on 2026-08-05 as the ultimate judge for crowgpt: BirdNET is
    circular (it selected the training corpus, so every detection number in the
    project carries that caveat), and Ben's ear costs an hour a sitting and is
    the bottleneck on the whole programme. A real crow answering a generated
    call would be the only instrument in the project that is not a proxy for the
    thing actually wanted.

    DECIDED NOT TO, by Ben, immediately and on two grounds:

      - It is unethical. Playback to a territorial, habituated group is a
        manipulation they did not consent to and cannot opt out of, and alarm
        playback especially imposes real stress for the experimenter's benefit.
      - It would burn the bridge. Habituation to these five birds is a one-way
        asset built over months. A group that learns it is being deceived by a
        speaker is a group whose responses to everything afterwards mean
        something different — so the playback that validated the generator would
        invalidate the field corpus it was validating it for.

    The second reason is the one worth keeping, because it is not only an ethical
    argument: trust is a measurement instrument here, and spending it on one
    reading destroys every future reading. It generalises past crows to any
    long-running observational relationship, and it is the reason not to reach
    for this later when the generator is better and the temptation is stronger.

    So the generator's ceiling instrument stays the blinded human sitting, and
    the way to make that cheaper is a learned critic validated against the 1,605
    recorded trials already on disk — not a louder speaker in the garden.


[ ] "Pokemon Crow" — gamified crowdsourcing. The instinct is right, the axis
    is wrong

    Ben, 2026-08-06: gamifying contribution is the future of the crowdsourcing
    half. Agreed on the premise and not on the mechanic, and the difference is
    worth writing down before anything is built, because a scoring rule is the
    hardest thing in a product to change later — people optimise for it
    immediately and resent losing it.

    WHAT A COLLECTION GAME WOULD REWARD, AND WHY EACH IS WRONG HERE:

      - Points for new birds or new call types is a standing incentive to make
        a bird do something. Approach it, call to it, play a recording, put
        food down. That is habituation sold as engagement, and it is the one
        instrument in this project that a single reading destroys — see the
        playback entry, which gates a *controlled* intervention behind an
        ethics decision. Crowdsourced uncontrolled intervention by strangers
        is the same act with none of the design and no way to count how often
        it happened.
      - Points attached to a label's content corrupts the only ground truth
        there is. `labels` is human testimony; a leaderboard for rare call
        types pays people to guess rare. Score the act of labelling if
        anything is scored, never the answer.
      - Volume of contributor audio is the thing the corpus already drowns in.
        Most of it is seeded and test material and the ★ Keep flag is the only
        thing separating the real from the rest — a flag that means "the
        recordist vouches for this", which does not survive being set by a
        stranger with a streak to protect. More unlabelled volume makes every
        aggregate less trustworthy, not more.
      - "Gotta catch 'em all" is a translation claim in a mascot costume. The
        outreach rule is that honest in-progress framing plays *better* with
        crow people and researchers, who are the data source.

    WHAT IS ACTUALLY WORTH GAMIFYING, on the evidence already in this file:

    Return, not capture. The confounding entry above is the strongest argument
    for recruiting anyone at all — every field recording was made by a feeder,
    so "a known feeder is present" is perfectly confounded with "a recording
    exists", and the valuable recruit is a `known_passerby`: someone who walks
    the same block daily, knows the birds, never feeds them. You do not retain
    that person with novelty. You retain them with *their* birds, over time.

    So the dex is a roster of individuals a contributor can personally
    identify, where an entry deepens by being recorded again across days and
    the streak is continuity rather than count. Same bird again is worth more
    than a new bird — which is the exact inverse of the Pokemon incentive, and
    the reason the name is a trap even if the shape is right. It also happens
    to be the shape the science needs: longitudinal, identified, one observer
    whose role is stable and recorded.

    The named characters are already the asset the outreach doc says they are —
    Edgar, Killer, Shredder, Thrasher, Ripjaw. A family page is the thing that
    travels, and it is a *product* of contributing rather than a score for it.

    And the scarce resource is labelling, not recording. `labels` was empty
    until recently, the label-set skill exists because getting testimony out of
    a recordist is the expensive part, and it is the one contribution that
    costs the birds nothing at all. If exactly one thing gets a progress bar,
    it is this.

    GATES, none of them new, all of them now load-bearing:

      - TLS and something other than one shared secret. Already flagged twice;
        a game makes it a blocker rather than a design question.
      - The same-app-or-different decision, which this forces. A field tool for
        recordists who know each other and a public game are not the same
        answer about identity or what a capture may carry.
      - Coordinates. A public dex of identified crows is a map of where named
        wild animals reliably are, at a resolution good enough to find them.
        `locationPrecision` and `censorCoords` stop being a courtesy.

    Not scheduled, and it should not be until the upload path exists and there
    is a second contributor at all. The part worth deciding early is only the
    scoring rule, because that is the part that cannot be walked back.

    ON A LEADERBOARD AS THE MINIMUM VERSION, added the same day. It is not the
    minimum. A single publicly ranked scalar is the highest distortion per line
    of code in the whole design space — a dex at least has many axes and no
    loser, a board has one number and everyone below the top. "Slightly
    gamified" describes the build cost, not the incentive.

    It also cannot exist yet, which is the practical answer. `contributors` has
    two rows: Ben (admin, all 61 labels) and the operator dashboard service
    account. A board would render one line.

    THE THING THAT DOES THE SAME JOB AT N=1 is a personal contribution view —
    your own numbers over time, days recorded, captures labelled, birds seen
    again this month, no comparison to anyone. It carries the whole retention
    argument, it is honest at one contributor and at a thousand, and a ranking
    is a later ORDER BY over the same query if it still seems wanted. Build
    that first regardless of where this lands.

    IF IT IS EVER RANKED, rank effort and not luck. Living next to a vocal
    family is luck; recording on a Tuesday is effort. Ben is permanently first
    on any capture- or bird-count board — his birds, his yard, daily — which
    makes every recruited neighbour permanently last, and the marginal
    contributor is exactly the person the confound entry says is worth having.
    Personal bests beat a global board wherever the contributors are
    structurally asymmetric, and here they always will be.

    Safe axes: days recorded, labelling streak, captures taken to fully
    labelled. Unsafe: birds identified, rare call types, and detection counts —
    the last ranks BirdNET's guesses rather than anything a human vouched for,
    which is the same "is this number about crows or about the pipeline"
    mistake the corpus already has to be defended against.


CROWGPT: "MORE DATA WILL NOT HELP" IS STATED HARDER THAN 0048 EARNED

    Ben, 2026-08-06: "I think I am data constrained. I know there is an
    experiment to the contrary but I don't trust it — we tested growing the
    corpus by 1.6x, which at my scale is not valid."

    Half of that is a misremembering and half of it is a real hole.

    THE 1.6x TEST IS 0050, AND HE IS RIGHT NOT TO TRUST IT — for a stronger
    reason than he gave. The corpus grew 57% but American Crow grew 4%
    (57,479 bouts to 59,654), because the archive's corvids are overwhelmingly
    European. 0050 is very nearly a null by construction and should not be
    cited as data evidence in either direction. Its own write-up says so.

    THE EVIDENCE IS 0048, AND IT IS AN 8x SWEEP, NOT 1.6x — 12.5/25/50/100% of
    recording groups, shared validation split, each doubling buying about a
    third of the previous one. That is a real scaling curve and the "small
    scale" objection does not land on it as stated.

    WHAT DOES LAND: every arm of 0048 ran at ONE model size, d384/L8/H6, 21.9M
    params. A scaling curve at fixed capacity flattens when the model
    saturates, which is not the same finding as the data being exhausted. 0048
    concludes "no model of any size trained on any amount of this corpus will
    do better" off four arms that varied one axis.

    AND THE CAPACITY RULING IT LEANS ON IS WEAKER THAN ITS SUMMARY TABLE SAYS.
    That table reads "capacity — ruled out twice, two sizes, two sequence
    lengths (0023, 0038)". But 0023 varied dropout, batch size and LR schedule
    and every arm was d384 — it never moved parameter count at all. 0038 did
    (16.6M vs 41M) and they scored 4/24 and 4/24, at five codebooks, where the
    incumbent scored 13/24. Two models tied at the floor cannot separate on
    capacity. So the standing evidence is one experiment that did not test the
    axis and one that tested it in a broken regime.

    THE DIAGONAL HAS THEREFORE NEVER BEEN RUN. Data varied at fixed capacity,
    capacity varied (badly) at fixed data, never both together — which is the
    move Chinchilla is actually about, and 0048 withdrew the Chinchilla
    argument without testing it.

    WHAT SURVIVES INTACT is 0048's train/val gap column, and it is the strongest
    thing in the file: +0.016 at 50% of the corpus. Checked — `eval_train_batcher`
    passes `canonical_only=True` and honours `keep_groups`, so this is not the
    augmentation mismatch that invalidated the older gap numbers. A data-starved
    model memorises; this one does not. That is a mechanism, not an
    extrapolation, and it is the reason to believe 0048 anyway.

    AND HIS UNDERLYING INTUITION IS ABOUT ABSOLUTE SCALE, which no experiment
    here has addressed. 41.7 hours, 74M unique tokens, against MusicGen's
    20,000 hours — 0.2%. Diminishing returns measured entirely inside
    18M-148M tokens is the local slope of a curve sampled three orders of
    magnitude below every system that works. Local flatness is what a power law
    looks like everywhere on it, including well below the point where the thing
    starts working.

    WHY IT CHANGES NOTHING PRACTICAL, WHICH IS THE HONEST BOTTOM LINE. If he is
    right, the implied action is 10-100x more American Crow, and it does not
    exist: 41.7 hours already IS the archive, a 10,637-recording sweep returned
    446 American Crows, and the kept field corpus is 8.6 minutes. So "data is
    the binding constraint" and "more data will not help" collapse to the same
    instruction — do not spend on the generator — by different routes.

    The difference is what gets written down. "More data will not help" invites
    another architecture idea. "The generator is out of reach at any data scale
    one person can reach" does not, and is better supported.

    IF IT IS EVER WORTH SETTLING: one arm, ~60M params at 100% data, against
    21.9M at 100% and 21.9M at 50%. Three points that turn two lines into a
    surface. About three hours. Worth it only if something else makes the
    generator matter again — 0048's real conclusion, that the honest pivot is
    the context work, is not disturbed by any of this.

[ ] Weather → activity prediction ("they'll be out at 10 AM")

    Raised 2026-08-20. Ben wants forecast-conditioned activity windows for
    the crows. The blocker is not weather data (Open-Meteo: free hourly
    history + forecast, no key) but ground truth: 21 kept captures with
    time+GPS, and capture timestamps measure WHEN BEN RECORDS, not when
    crows are active — selection bias a model trained on them inherits
    silently. Three-step path, cheapest first:

    1. Start the flywheel now: enrich every capture at ingest with the
       hour's weather (temp, wind, precip, cloud, pressure) + backfill the
       existing kept 21 from the historical API. Costs one worker touch;
       accumulates free forever; makes every future analysis joinable.
    2. The real unlock is the roof station (Jay's Song Meter assessment,
       phases/roof-station.md): continuous BirdNET detections x hourly
       weather = unbiased activity curves in weeks, thousands of hours.
       Activity prediction should be DESIGNED against that stream; capture
       timestamps can validate but never train.
    3. Until fitted: a dashboard "crow weather" card scoring tomorrow's
       forecast against literature priors (crepuscular peaks, midday lull
       in heat, wind suppression), clearly labelled as priors — replaced by
       the fitted model when station data exists. Makes the idea tangible
       without pretending 21 points are a dataset.

[ ] Road noise as a CawScout axis — recordability, not a crow penalty

    Ben, 2026-08-21, standing at target #4 with three crows and a busy road:
